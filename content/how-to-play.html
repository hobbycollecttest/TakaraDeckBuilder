New to HORO-BEAT? These twelve steps cover everything you need for your first game. The pictures are the How to Play series from Horo-Beat Card Game SEA.

When you want the full detail, read the comprehensive rules and the FAQ.

1. What is HORO-BEAT?

The HORO-BEAT Card Game is a supernatural battle TCG. You fight alongside your Horobito and use Waza in battles.

2. Your deck

A deck has 50 cards. You can use up to 4 copies of each card, counted by card name. The deck builder checks this for you.

3. The battlefield

Your side of the table has a Heart Zone, a Battle Line with five lines (1st to 5th), a Waza Zone, an Ena Zone, your deck, your Graveyard and your hand.

4. Setting up

  1. Shuffle your deck and put it on the deck position.
  2. Play rock-paper-scissors to decide who goes first and second in the first round.
  3. Draw 6 cards. This is your hand.
  4. Look at your hand and decide if you want to redraw. If you do, return all 6 cards to your deck, shuffle again and draw 6 new cards.
  5. Put the top 5 cards of your deck face down into the Heart Zone.
  6. Put the top card of your deck face up into your Ena Zone.

5. How a round goes

  • Summon Phase: first player, then second player. Place a card from your hand into Ena, then place Horobito on the line. You can place several Horobito in the same round as long as you have the Ena.
  • Battle Phase: starting from the 1st line, the Horobito fight each other in one-on-one duels.
  • End Phase: draw a card for each unused Ena you have. This is called Ena Draw. Recover your used Ena and go to the next round. The player who won at the 1st line goes first in the next round.

How to win: destroy all of your opponent's Hearts, then deal the final damage.

6. Cost and Ena

To use a card you pay its cost with Ena. You start the game with 1 Ena, and once per round you can place a card from your hand into the Ena Zone. The more Ena you build up, the stronger the Horobito and Waza you can use.

After you use an Ena, turn it sideways to show it is used. Used Ena stand back up at the end of each round, so feel free to spend them.

7. Ena Draw

Ending the round with unused Ena is not a waste. For every unused Ena you have at the end of the round, you draw a card from your deck.

8. The Battle Line

This is where the Horobito clash. Battles happen in order of the Battle Line. The two Horobito facing each other compare their Power. If there is no Horobito across from yours, your Horobito wins by walkover.

9. Damage

When you lose a battle, your Horobito and a Heart take damage. A damaged card is put in the downed position (sideways). A card that takes damage while it is already downed is destroyed. Destroyed Horobito go to the Graveyard.

10. Hearts and Triggers

When a Heart is destroyed, add it to your hand. When you do, the card's Trigger activates. There are different kinds of Trigger depending on the card, so use it as written. This is your chance to turn the game around.

11. Waza and Boost

When two Horobito battle, you can use Waza and Boost to raise Power. Both need Ena. In the example, a Horobito about to lose gets +2000 from a Boost and +5000 from a Waza, and wins the battle.

12. Overbeat

When you place a Horobito onto the Battle Line on top of one you already have there, its cost goes down by the cost of the Horobito underneath. This is Overbeat. For example, a cost 7 Horobito placed over a cost 2 Horobito costs 5.

A Horobito placed with Overbeat enters undamaged, even if the one underneath was damaged.
