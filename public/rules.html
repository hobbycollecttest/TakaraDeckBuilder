HORO-BEAT Comprehensive Rules in English

A lot of you have been asking for the full HORO-BEAT rules in English, so here they are. This is our translation of the official Japanese Comprehensive Rules from Takara Tomy, version 26.11.14.

You don't need all of this to start playing. It's mostly for judges, tournament players and anyone who wants to know exactly how a tricky interaction works. If you just have a quick question, check our HORO-BEAT FAQ first.

Version 26.11.14 · Established November 14, 2026

Unofficial English translation of the official Japanese Comprehensive Rules published by Takara Tomy. If this translation and the Japanese original ever disagree, the Japanese original takes precedence.

1. Game Overview

1.1. Number of Players

1.1.1. These rules describe a game played between 2 players.

1.2. Basic Principles

1.2.1. If the rules and a card's text contradict each other, the card's text takes precedence.

1.2.2. If an effect that allows something contradicts an effect that prevents it, the effect that prevents it takes precedence.

1.3. Winning and Losing

1.3.1. If a player meets any of the following conditions, that player loses and the game ends immediately.

  • 1.3.1.1. A player with no Hearts takes 1 or more damage.
    • 1.3.1.1.1. Dealing this damage is called a Finishing Blow.
    • 1.3.1.1.2. Taking damage that "cannot deal a Finishing Blow" does not make a player lose.
  • 1.3.1.2. A player with no cards in their deck begins their Draw Step.

1.3.2. If both players meet a losing condition at the same time, the game ends in a draw.

1.3.3. When the game ends, if one player has won, the other player loses. If one player has lost, the other player wins.

1.4. Conceding

1.4.1. Either player may concede at any time during the game. The conceding player loses and the game ends. Conceding cannot be affected by any effect, and no effect can be applied to a loss by concession.

2. Card Information

2.1. Card Name

2.1.1. The text that names the card. Cards with the same name are treated as the same card.

2.2. Card Type

2.2.1. The text that shows the card's type. Every card is either a Horobeat or a Waza.

  • 2.2.1.1. A Horobeat is a card type that is played onto the Battle Line.
  • 2.2.1.2. A Waza is a card type that is used during battle.

2.3. Cost

2.3.1. The number showing the card's cost: how many Ena you need to play it. If a cost would ever drop below 0 for any reason, it is treated as 0 instead.

2.4. Color

2.4.1. The card's color. A card has 0 or more of the colors Red, Blue, and Green. Color is checked when paying the card's cost.

2.4.2. A card's color is shown as the background color of its cost.

2.5. Tribe

2.5.1. Text on the card that card effects may refer to.

2.6. Text

2.6.1. Overview

2.6.1.1. The text describing the card's effects and similar information.

2.6.2. Conditions

2.6.2.1. Requirements that must be met to use the card.

2.6.2.2. Written as "Can only be used when…", "Can only be played when…", or "Cannot be played if…".

2.6.3. Effects

2.6.3.1. Any text other than conditions that affects the game.

2.6.3.2. A card's text may contain multiple effects. If several effects on one card happen at the same time, they are applied from top to bottom.

2.6.4. Conditional Effects

2.6.4.1. Some text pairs a condition, such as "Ena Reading [Color] X" or "Resonance: [Tribe]", with an effect. This is called a conditional effect.

2.6.4.2. A conditional effect only works while the condition at its start is met.

2.6.5. Reminder Text

2.6.5.1. Card text may include sentences in parentheses, mainly to explain an effect.

2.6.5.2. Reminder text generally has no meaning in the game.

2.6.6. Trigger

2.6.6.1. Describes an effect you may apply when this card is destroyed from your Hearts.

2.6.6.2. A Trigger is an effect of that card.

2.7. Power

2.7.1. Horobeat Power

  • 2.7.1.1. A number that Horobeat have.
  • 2.7.1.2. Mainly used in battles between Horobeat.
  • 2.7.1.3. If a Horobeat's power would ever drop below 0 for any reason, it is treated as 0 instead.

2.7.2. Waza Power

  • 2.7.2.1. A number that Waza have.
  • 2.7.2.2. When you use the Waza, its power is added to your Horobeat's power.

2.8. Boost

2.8.1. Overview

  • 2.8.1.1. Information some Horobeat have.
  • 2.8.1.2. A Boost includes a cost and a power.
  • 2.8.1.3. A Horobeat with a Boost can also be used as a Boost during the Battle Phase.

2.8.2. Boost Cost

2.8.2.1. The cost paid when using the card as a Boost.

2.8.3. Boost Power

2.8.3.1. The power used when the card is used as a Boost.

2.9. Controller

2.9.1. The player tied to the zone the card is in.

2.9.2. This isn't printed on the card; it is determined by which zone the card is in.

2.10. Owner

2.10.1. The player whose deck the card was in at the start of the game.

2.10.2. This isn't printed on the card.

2.11. Rarity

2.11.1. The card's rarity.

2.11.2. It has no meaning in the game.

2.12. Card Number

2.12.1. Text that identifies the card. It has no meaning in the game.

2.13. Illustrator

2.13.1. Text naming the card's illustrator. It has no meaning in the game.

2.14. Copyright Notice

2.14.1. Text showing the game's copyright. It has no meaning in the game.

3. Card States

3.1. Overview

3.1.1. Depending on the zone, a card is either Downed / Upright or Used / Unused.

3.1.2. A card's state is shown by how it is placed.

3.1.3. What a state means depends on the zone.

3.2. Upright

3.2.1. A card in this state is placed vertically.

3.2.2. It represents a Horobeat or Heart that hasn't taken damage.

3.2.3. Putting a Horobeat or Heart into this state is called Recovering it.

  • 3.2.3.1. Even if a Horobeat or Heart was already upright, it still counts as "recovered" when an effect recovers it.

3.3. Downed

3.3.1. A card in this state is placed horizontally.

3.3.2. It represents a Horobeat or Heart that has taken damage.

3.3.3. Putting a Horobeat or Heart into this state is called Downing it.

3.4. Unused

3.4.1. A card in this state is placed vertically.

3.4.2. It represents Ena that can be used to pay costs.

3.4.3. Putting Ena into this state is called making it unused.

3.5. Used

3.5.1. A card in this state is placed horizontally.

3.5.2. It represents Ena that can't be used to pay costs.

3.5.3. Putting Ena into this state is called making it used.

4. Zones

4.1. All Zones

4.1.1. During the game, every card is in a zone.

4.1.2. Unless stated otherwise, each player has their own separate copy of each zone.

4.1.3. Some zones show card information to all players and some don't. The former are public zones; the latter are hidden zones.

  • 4.1.3.1. Unless stated otherwise, cards in public zones are face up and cards in hidden zones are face down.

4.1.4. When a zone has multiple cards, either player may check how many there are at any time.

4.2. Moving Cards Between Zones

4.2.1. When multiple cards move to another zone, unless stated otherwise, the cards' owner decides the order they're placed in.

4.2.2. When multiple cards move to a hidden zone, unless stated otherwise, the player who doesn't own them can't know their order.

4.2.3. If you're told to move cards from a zone that doesn't have enough cards, move as many as you can.

4.3. Order

4.3.1. Zones that hold multiple cards keep those cards in an order.

4.3.2. Order is shown by stacking the cards on top of each other.

4.4. Deck

4.4.1. The zone where you place your deck at the start of the game.

4.4.2. This is a hidden zone. Unless stated otherwise, neither player can look at the faces of these cards.

4.4.3. Multiple cards in this zone are stacked.

4.4.4. Unless stated otherwise, the order of cards in this zone can't be changed.

4.4.5. When told to shuffle your deck, put the cards in this zone into a random order.

4.4.6. When told to draw cards, move that many cards from the top of this zone to your hand.

4.5. Hand

4.5.1. The zone for the cards in your hand.

4.5.2. This is a hidden zone.

4.5.3. You may look at your own hand at any time and rearrange it freely.

4.5.4. You may hold the cards in your hand.

4.6. Heart Zone

4.6.1. The zone for the cards that serve as a player's Hearts.

4.6.2. This is a hidden zone. Unless stated otherwise, neither player can look at the faces of these cards.

4.6.3. Multiple cards in this zone are stacked slightly offset so the count is visible.

4.6.4. Cards in this zone are called Hearts.

4.6.5. Cards in this zone are either downed or upright.

4.6.6. Unless stated otherwise, the order of cards in this zone can't be changed.

4.7. Check Zone

4.7.1. The zone where cards destroyed from your Hearts are placed temporarily.

4.7.2. This is a hidden zone.

4.7.3. However, you may look at your own Check Zone at any time and rearrange it freely.

4.8. Graveyard

4.8.1. The zone for cards that have finished being used.

4.8.2. This is a public zone.

4.8.3. Either player may look at the faces of cards in this zone at any time and rearrange them freely.

4.8.4. Multiple cards in this zone are stacked.

4.9. Battle Line

4.9.1. Overview

4.9.1.1. The Battle Line has 5 lines lined up side by side.

4.9.2. Lines

4.9.2.1. Each line has one Horobeat Zone and one Waza Zone for each player.

4.9.2.2. Each line has a line number based on its position.

4.9.2.3. Lines are called Line 1, Line 2 … Line 5.

4.9.2.4. The opponent's zone with the same line number is sometimes called directly opposite.

4.9.2.5. From any given line, lines with a lower number are inner lines and lines with a higher number are outer lines.

4.9.3. Horobeat Zone

4.9.3.1. The zone for Horobeat you've summoned.

4.9.3.2. This is a public zone.

4.9.3.3. Either player may look at the faces of these cards at any time.

4.9.3.4. Cards in this zone are either downed or upright.

4.9.3.5. Multiple cards in this zone are stacked.

4.9.3.6. Moving a Horobeat also moves the cards stacked under it, keeping their order and states.

  • 4.9.3.6.1. However, if they move to a zone without states, they lose their states.

4.9.3.7. If one Horobeat Zone has multiple cards, only the top card is treated as the Horobeat.

  • 4.9.3.7.1. In this case, only the top card has a state; the other cards have none.

4.9.3.8. Unless stated otherwise, the order of cards in this zone can't be changed.

4.9.4. Waza Zone

4.9.4.1. The zone for Waza used and Horobeat used as a Boost during that line's battle.

4.9.4.2. This is a public zone.

4.9.4.3. Either player may look at the faces of these cards at any time and rearrange them freely.

4.9.4.4. The power of Waza in this zone, and the Boost Power of Horobeat used as a Boost, are always added to the power of the same player's Horobeat in that line's Horobeat Zone.

4.10. Ena Zone

4.10.1. The zone where cards are placed as Ena.

4.10.2. This is a public zone. Either player may look at the faces of these cards at any time.

4.10.3. You may rearrange the cards in this zone freely, but you may not change their states while doing so.

4.10.4. Cards in this zone are either used or unused.

5. Game Setup

5.1. Deck Preparation

5.1.1. Each player prepares a deck of exactly 50 cards.

5.1.2. A deck may contain up to 4 cards with the same name.

5.2. Pre-Game Setup

5.2.1. Each player shuffles their deck thoroughly.

5.2.2. Play rock-paper-scissors. The winner is the first First Player.

  • 5.2.2.1. At this time, decide whether Line 1 is on the left or the right from the First Player's view.

5.2.3. Each player draws 6 cards.

  • 5.2.3.1. After looking at their hand, each player may declare a mulligan once.
  • 5.2.3.2. To mulligan, return your whole hand to your deck, shuffle it, and draw 6 cards.

5.2.4. Each player places the top 5 cards of their deck upright as Hearts.

5.2.5. Each player places the top card of their deck into their Ena Zone, unused.

6. Game Progression

6.1. Rounds

6.1.1. The game is played as a series of rounds.

6.1.2. A round is made up of the Summon Phase, Battle Phase, and End Phase, in that order.

6.1.3. At all times during the game, one player is the First Player and the other is the Second Player.

6.2. Summon Phase

6.2.1. Summon Phase Procedure

6.2.1.1. Starting with the First Player, each player takes one Summon Phase in turn.

6.2.1.2. Each player's Summon Phase is made up of the Draw Step, Ena Charge Step, and Main Step, in that order.

6.2.2. Draw Step

6.2.2.1. If the player taking their Draw Step has 0 cards in their deck, that player loses.

  • 6.2.2.1.1. Having 0 cards in your deck at any other time does not make you lose.

6.2.2.2. The player taking their Draw Step draws 1 card.

6.2.2.3. This step is skipped in the first round of the game.

6.2.3. Ena Charge Step

6.2.4. The player taking their Ena Charge Step may choose 1 card from their hand and place it into their Ena Zone, unused.

6.2.5. Main Step

6.2.5.1. The player taking their Main Step may play 1 Horobeat from their hand. They may repeat this as many times as they like.

  • 6.2.5.1.1. Horobeat can't be used as a Boost during the Summon Phase.

6.2.5.2. When the player is done playing cards, the Main Step ends.

  • 6.2.5.2.1. When the Main Step ends, that player's "when the Summon Phase ends" effects activate.

6.2.6. Playing a Horobeat

6.2.6.1. There are 2 ways to play a Horobeat: play it to a new line or Overbeat.

6.2.6.2. Decide which Horobeat in your hand to play and how, then follow that method's steps.

  • 6.2.6.2.1. You must make a choice that lets you pay the cost.
  • 6.2.6.2.2. If the Horobeat's text has a condition for playing it, it can't be played unless the condition is met.

6.2.6.3. Unless stated otherwise, a played Horobeat enters its line upright.

6.2.6.4. Playing to a new line means placing the Horobeat in the innermost line that doesn't have one of your Horobeat.

  • 6.2.6.4.1. If you played a Horobeat from your hand as part of your Main Step, pay its cost.
    • 6.2.6.4.1.1. When an effect puts a Horobeat into a new line, you don't pay its cost unless stated otherwise.
  • 6.2.6.4.2. Place the Horobeat in your Horobeat Zone of the innermost line that doesn't have one of your Horobeat.
  • 6.2.6.4.3. If the Horobeat has a "when this Horobeat is played" effect, it activates.
  • 6.2.6.4.4. If every line already has one of your Horobeat, you can't play a Horobeat to a new line.

6.2.6.5. Overbeat means playing a Horobeat on top of one of your Horobeat.

  • 6.2.6.5.1. Choose one of your Horobeat already on the Battle Line. This is the Overbeat base.
    • 6.2.6.5.1.1. You must choose a Horobeat whose cost is lower than the cost of the Horobeat you're playing with Overbeat. If either the base or the Horobeat being played has a cost requirement for this, ignore this rule and follow that requirement instead.
    • 6.2.6.5.1.2. If there's no Horobeat you can choose, you can't play a Horobeat this way.
  • 6.2.6.5.2. If you played a Horobeat from your hand as part of your Main Step, pay its cost.
    • 6.2.6.5.2.1. Under the cost-payment rules, the cost is reduced by the cost of the Overbeat base.
    • 6.2.6.5.2.2. When an effect makes you Overbeat, you don't pay the cost unless stated otherwise.
  • 6.2.6.5.3. Place the Horobeat on top of the Overbeat base.
  • 6.2.6.5.4. Activate the new Horobeat's "when this Horobeat is played" effects and the base's "when this Horobeat is Overbeaten" effects, in any order. The player who played the Horobeat chooses the order.
  • 6.2.6.5.5. A Horobeat played this way is treated as a different Horobeat from its Overbeat base.

6.3. Battle Phase

6.3.1. Battle Phase Procedure

6.3.1.1. Starting from Line 1 and moving outward, repeat the line procedure below for each line.

6.3.1.2. The line procedure is only carried out for lines where at least one player has a Horobeat.

6.3.2. Line Procedure

6.3.2.1. The steps below, from comparing the Horobeat's power through deciding the result, are called a battle.

  • 6.3.2.1.1. If a "no battle takes place" effect applies to the line, skip everything up to "the battle is considered over" in 6.3.2.13.

6.3.2.2. If a Horobeat in the line has a "when the battle starts" effect, it activates.

6.3.2.3. Starting with the First Player, players take turns making Battle Phase actions.

6.3.2.4. When one player passes and the other player passes right after, move on to the next step.

6.3.2.5. If both players have a Horobeat in the line, compare their power and decide the battle as follows.

  • 6.3.2.5.1. The side with higher power wins the battle.
  • 6.3.2.5.2. The side with lower power loses the battle.
  • 6.3.2.5.3. If the power is equal, the battle is a draw.
  • 6.3.2.5.4. Unless an effect refers to states, power is compared regardless of state.

6.3.2.6. If the top card of a Horobeat in the line changes after this point, that top card's "when this wins a battle", "when this loses a battle", and "when this Horobeat's battle ends" effects don't activate during this line's procedure.

6.3.2.7. If only one player has a Horobeat in the line, that Horobeat wins the battle.

6.3.2.8. If a Horobeat that battled has a "when this wins a battle" effect, it activates.

6.3.2.9. If a Horobeat that battled has a "when this loses a battle" effect, it activates.

6.3.2.10. Work out the damage to Horobeat and players.

  • 6.3.2.10.1. A Horobeat that won or drew will deal 1 damage to the opposing Horobeat.
  • 6.3.2.10.2. A Horobeat that won will deal 1 damage to the opposing player.
  • 6.3.2.10.3. Apply any effects that change damage dealt to Horobeat or players when working this out.
    • 6.3.2.10.3.1. For example, Double Damage or an activated X Barrier.
  • 6.3.2.10.4. Unless an effect refers to states, damage is worked out regardless of state.

6.3.2.11. Deal the calculated damage to the Horobeat.

6.3.2.12. Deal the calculated damage to the players.

6.3.2.13. The line's battle is considered over. If a Horobeat in the line has a "when this battle ends" or "when this Horobeat's battle ends" effect, it activates.

6.3.2.14. Move all cards in each player's Waza Zone in this line to the Graveyard.

  • 6.3.2.14.1. This step and the ones after it still happen even if a "no battle takes place" effect applies to the line.

6.3.2.15. If there is a line further out where either player has a Horobeat, repeat the line procedure for the next line out.

6.3.2.16. If there are no Horobeat further out for either player, the Battle Phase ends.

6.3.3. Battle Phase Actions

6.3.3.1. A Battle Phase action means choosing one of the following:

  • 6.3.3.1.1. Use a Waza
  • 6.3.3.1.2. Boost
  • 6.3.3.1.3. Pass

6.3.3.2. A player with no Horobeat in the line can't choose "Use a Waza" or "Boost".

6.3.4. Using a Waza

6.3.4.1. Decide which Waza in your hand to use.

  • 6.3.4.1.1. You must make a choice that lets you pay the cost.
  • 6.3.4.1.2. If the Waza has a [condition], it can't be used unless the condition is met.

6.3.4.2. When using a Waza as a Battle Phase action, pay its cost.

6.3.4.3. Place the Waza sideways in the Waza Zone of the line being resolved.

  • 6.3.4.3.1. The power of a Waza in a player's Waza Zone is always added to the power of that player's Horobeat in the same line.
  • 6.3.4.3.2. That player's Horobeat in the line is considered to have "used a Waza".

6.3.4.4. If the Waza has a "when used" effect, it activates.

6.3.4.5. If any Horobeat have effects that activate when a Waza is used, they activate.

6.3.5. Boosting

6.3.5.1. Decide which Horobeat with a Boost in your hand to use as a Boost.

  • 6.3.5.1.1. You must make a choice that lets you pay the Boost Cost.
  • 6.3.5.1.2. Even when Boosting, you must meet the color requirement when paying.

6.3.5.2. When Boosting as a Battle Phase action, pay the Horobeat's Boost Cost.

6.3.5.3. Place the Horobeat sideways in the Waza Zone of the line being resolved.

  • 6.3.5.3.1. The Boost Power of a Horobeat in a player's Waza Zone is always added to the power of that player's Horobeat in the same line.
  • 6.3.5.3.2. That player's Horobeat in the line is considered to have "had a Boost used on it".

6.3.5.4. Using a Horobeat as a Boost does not meet a "when a Waza is used" condition.

  • 6.3.5.4.1. It does meet conditions that explicitly include Boosts, such as "when a Waza or Boost is used" or "when a Boost is used".

6.3.6. Pass

6.3.6.1. Do nothing.

6.3.7. Switching First Player

6.3.7.1. After every line's battle has ended, do the following.

6.3.7.2. The player who won the battle in Line 1 this Battle Phase becomes the First Player next round.

6.3.7.3. If the battle in Line 1 was a draw or didn't happen, the First Player and Second Player switch.

6.4. End Phase

6.4.1. End Phase Procedure

6.4.1.1. Power changes to Horobeat from effects other than continuous effects stop applying.

6.4.1.2. Then both players carry out Switching First Player, the Slide Step, the Ena Draw Step, and the Ena Refresh Step, in that order.

6.4.2. Slide Step

6.4.2.1. If you have a Horobeat in a line and a line further in has none of your Horobeat, move that Horobeat to the innermost line without a Horobeat.

  • 6.4.2.1.1. This is called sliding.

6.4.3. Ena Draw Step

6.4.3.1. Each player draws cards equal to the number of unused cards in their own Ena Zone.

6.4.4. Ena Refresh Step

6.4.4.1. Each player makes all cards in their own Ena Zone unused.

7. Rule Processes

7.1. Overview

7.1.1. Processes the rules carry out automatically in specific situations during the game.

7.2. Paying Costs

7.2.1. When paying a cost, check the card's color and cost.

7.2.2. Choose unused cards in your Ena Zone equal to the cost.

  • 7.2.2.1. Together, the cards chosen from your Ena Zone (or the Overbeat base) must include every color of the card you're paying for.
  • 7.2.2.2. If you can't choose enough cards from your Ena Zone under this rule, you can't pay the cost.
  • 7.2.2.3. If you can't pay the cost, you can't declare that you're using the card.
  • 7.2.2.4. When playing a Horobeat with Overbeat, the number of Ena you need to choose is reduced by the Overbeat base's cost.

7.2.3. If you can pay the cost, make all the chosen Ena used.

7.3. Damage to Horobeat

7.3.1. When 1 or more damage is dealt to a Horobeat, it counts as damage dealt to that Horobeat, and the following happens.

7.3.2. If any effects trigger "when a Horobeat takes damage", they activate.

7.3.3. Then, depending on the damaged Horobeat's state:

  • 7.3.3.1. If the Horobeat was downed, destroy it.
  • 7.3.3.2. If the Horobeat was upright, down it.
    • 7.3.3.2.1. Then, if any effects trigger "when downed by damage", they activate.

7.4. Destroying Horobeat

7.4.1. When a Horobeat is destroyed, put it into the Graveyard.

  • 7.4.1.1. If other cards are stacked under it, put them into the Graveyard at the same time.

7.4.2. If any effects trigger "when destroyed", they activate.

  • 7.4.2.1. However, cards stacked under the destroyed Horobeat are not considered "destroyed".
  • 7.4.2.2. If a card stacked under a Horobeat is hit by a "destroy the card under a Horobeat" effect, its "when this Horobeat is destroyed" effect doesn't activate, even if it has one.

7.4.3. If a Horobeat with no cards under it is hit by a "destroy the bottom card of a Horobeat" effect, that Horobeat is destroyed.

  • 7.4.3.1. If that Horobeat has an effect that triggers "when destroyed", it activates.

7.5. Damage to Players

7.5.1. If a player with no Hearts takes 1 or more damage, that player loses the game.

  • 7.5.1.1. This is called a Finishing Blow.

7.5.2. If a player with Hearts takes 1 or more damage, all of it is dealt to their Hearts.

7.6. Damage to Hearts

7.6.1. Depending on the state of the top card of the damaged player's Heart Zone:

  • 7.6.1.1. If the top card is downed, destroy it.
  • 7.6.1.2. If the top card is upright, down it.

7.6.2. Repeat this once for each point of damage.

  • 7.6.2.1. However, if the player runs out of Hearts during this process, stop dealing damage to them.

7.6.3. If Heart damage is dealt to a player with no Hearts, nothing happens.

7.7. Destroying Hearts

7.7.1. Procedure

7.7.1.1. When a Heart is destroyed, move it to the Check Zone. Carry out the Check process as soon as the damage to the player or their Hearts finishes.

  • 7.7.1.1.1. Don't carry out the Check process while damage to a player or their Hearts is still being dealt.

7.7.2. Check Process

7.7.2.1. The player carrying out the Check chooses 1 card in their Check Zone.

  • 7.7.2.1.1. You can choose the order regardless of the order the cards were destroyed in.

7.7.2.2. If that card has a Trigger effect, you may activate it.

  • 7.7.2.2.1. If you choose to activate it, reveal the card, then activate its Trigger effect.

7.7.2.3. If you don't activate a Trigger effect, put the card into its owner's hand.

7.7.2.4. If cards remain in the Check Zone, repeat the Check process.

7.8. Activating Effects

7.8.1. Some effects activate under specific conditions.

7.8.2. When an effect activates, resolve it.

  • 7.8.2.1. If an effect can't be fully carried out and only part of it is possible, do as much as you can.

7.8.3. If effects from cards with different controllers activate at the same time, resolve all the First Player's effects first, then all the Second Player's effects.

  • 7.8.3.1. Effects with the same owner are resolved in the order set out in 7.8.4.
  • 7.8.3.2. If a First Player's effect activates while a Second Player's effect is being resolved, the First Player's effect is resolved first.

7.8.4. If effects from cards with the same controller activate at the same time, that player chooses which to resolve first. After one is resolved, they choose the next.

  • 7.8.4.1. If a new effect activates from a card with the same owner during this, it is treated like the other waiting effects and can be chosen next.

7.9. Replacement Effects

7.9.1. Some effects tell you to do something else instead of a specific process.

7.9.2. They're usually written as "instead, …".

7.9.3. Each replacement effect can be applied to a given process at most once.

7.9.4. If multiple replacement effects apply to one process, the First Player chooses and applies one of their own. They repeat this until none of theirs can be applied, then the Second Player does the same.

  • 7.9.4.1. If replacing a process changes which replacement effects can apply, follow 7.9.4 using the updated set.

8. Effects

8.1. Effect Categories

8.1.1. Every effect is a triggered effect, a continuous effect, or a Waza on-use effect.

8.1.2. A triggered effect activates under a specific condition. It's usually written as "When X, …".

  • 8.1.2.1. Triggered effects activate.
  • 8.1.2.2. If, while resolving an effect triggered by a condition, another effect triggered by the same condition becomes active, that other effect also activates.
    (Example: You have effect A, an end-of-battle effect whose Ena Reading condition isn't met, and effect B, an end-of-battle effect that adds Ena. If effect A's Ena Reading condition is met while resolving effect B, effect A activates after effect B resolves.)
  • 8.1.2.3. If, while resolving an effect triggered by a condition, another waiting effect triggered by the same condition stops being active, that other effect doesn't resolve.

8.1.3. A continuous effect keeps applying as long as a specific condition is met. It's usually written as "As long as X, …".

  • 8.1.3.1. Continuous effects don't activate.

8.1.4. A Waza on-use effect is written on a Waza and activates when the Waza is used.

  • 8.1.4.1. Waza on-use effects activate.

8.1.5. Some common conditions use the special notation shown below.

8.2. Omitted Controller

8.2.1. Effect or condition text sometimes doesn't say whose card or zone it means. In that case, it means your card or zone.

8.2.2. In text written as "Your opponent does X", if X doesn't say whose card or zone it means, it means your opponent's card or zone.

8.2.3. In text written as "Each player does X", if X doesn't say whose card or zone it means, each player does it separately with their own cards or zones.

8.3. Trigger Effects

8.3.1. Overview

8.3.1.1. Triggered effects that can activate when a card in your Hearts is destroyed.

8.3.2. Entry Trigger

8.3.2.1. You may play this Horobeat to a new line or with Overbeat.

8.3.2.2. Don't check whether you can pay the cost, and don't pay it.

8.3.3. Over Trigger

8.3.3.1. You may play this Horobeat with Overbeat.

8.3.3.2. Don't check whether you can pay the cost, and don't pay it.

8.3.4. Draw Trigger

8.3.4.1. Put this card into the Graveyard and draw 3 cards.

8.3.5. Last Over Trigger

8.3.5.1. Reveal this card, add it to your hand, and draw 1 card. Then, if you have 1 or fewer Hearts at that point, you may play this Horobeat with Overbeat.

8.3.5.2. Don't check whether you can pay the cost, and don't pay it.

8.4. Keyword Effects

8.4.1. Overview

8.4.1.1. Keyword effects are a shorthand for effects with specific processes.

8.4.2. Double Damage

8.4.2.1. When this Horobeat wins a battle, it deals 2 damage to the opponent instead of 1.

8.4.3. X Barrier

8.4.3.1. An effect that activates when this Horobeat loses a battle. Reduce the damage its player takes in that battle by X.

  • 8.4.3.1.1. This still applies even if the Horobeat is destroyed in that battle.

9. Specific Terms

9.1. Recover / Down

9.1.1. Puts a card in a zone that uses the downed state into the stated state.

9.1.2. Recovering a card that's already upright, or downing a card that's already downed, doesn't change its state.

9.1.3. Even if the state doesn't change, the card still counts as downed or recovered, and effects that trigger on that will activate.

9.1.4. The same applies to making Ena "unused" or "used".

9.2. Reveal

9.2.1. Some effects or processes tell you to reveal a card.

9.2.2. For the duration of that effect or process, let all players see the card's information.

9.2.3. When the effect or process ends, return the card to how it was.

9.3. Playing a Horobeat

9.3.1. Placing a Horobeat card from another zone into a Horobeat Zone so it's treated as a Horobeat.

9.3.2. Unless stated otherwise, a played Horobeat is upright.

9.4. Swapping Horobeat

9.4.1. This names 2 Horobeat. Call them A and B.

9.4.2. Move B to A's line, and move A to B's line.

9.5. Overbeat Base

9.5.1. In an Overbeat, the Horobeat underneath is called the Overbeat base.

9.6. Cannot Be Overbeaten

9.6.1. When playing a Horobeat with Overbeat, you can't choose to place it on a Horobeat this effect applies to.

9.7. Power +X

9.7.1. Adds X to the stated Horobeat's power.

9.8. Ena Reading [Color] X

9.8.1. Text a card has as the condition for a conditional effect.

9.8.2. The condition is met if the card's controller has X or more cards of the stated [Color] in their Ena Zone.

9.9. Resonance: [Tribe]

9.9.1. Text a Waza has as the condition for a conditional effect.

9.9.2. The condition is met if the Horobeat in the line where the Waza is used has [Tribe] as part of its tribe.

9.10. Effects That Move the Top Card of a Horobeat

9.10.1. Effects that move only a Horobeat's top card, such as "return the top card of a Horobeat to your hand".

9.10.2. If such an effect leaves a card on top that isn't a Horobeat, all cards in that Horobeat Zone are put into the Graveyard.

9.10.3. If such an effect leaves another Horobeat on top, it stays in place and keeps the downed or upright state of the card that was moved away.

  • 9.10.3.1. All other information now comes from the new top card.

9.10.4. This effect can also apply to a Horobeat with no cards under it.

9.10.5. This does not count as "a Horobeat entering a line".

9.11. Cannot Be Destroyed

9.11.1. A Horobeat with a "cannot be destroyed" effect isn't destroyed when it takes damage while downed, and isn't affected by "destroy" effects.

9.11.2. A Horobeat with a "cannot be destroyed" effect isn't affected by "destroy the top card of a Horobeat".

9.11.3. If a Horobeat with a "cannot be destroyed" effect has cards under it, it is affected by "destroy the card under a Horobeat".

  • 9.11.3.1. If it has no cards under it, it isn't affected by "destroy the card under a Horobeat".
  • 9.11.3.2. If a card hit by "destroy the card under a Horobeat" has a "this Horobeat cannot be destroyed" effect, that effect doesn't apply and the card is destroyed.

9.12. Using a Waza

9.12.1. When a card effect has you use a Waza, you use it without paying its cost unless the effect says otherwise.
