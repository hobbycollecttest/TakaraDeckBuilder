How to Play Haikyu!! VOBACA!! BREAK

This is our plain-English guide to playing Haikyu!! VOBACA!! BREAK, the volleyball card game by Takara Tomy. It is written by Hobby Collect in our own words to help new players get started. It is not the official rulebook. Where the official English wording exists we use it, so the terms here match the English cards.

For official rulings, always check the rules on the official English rules page. If this guide and the official rules ever disagree, the official rules win.

What kind of game is it?

Two players play a volleyball match with cards. One player serves, and then the ball goes back and forth. On your turn you either block the ball or receive it, set it and attack it back. The rally goes on until one player cannot return the ball. That player loses the set. Lose three sets and you lose the match.

Your deck

  • A deck is exactly 40 cards.
  • You can use at most 8 Event cards.
  • You can use any number of copies of the same card.

The deck builder checks all three for you.

The two card types

Character cards

Characters are your players. Each one has five numbers, called parameters: Serve Point, Block Point, Receive Point, Set Point and Attack Point. A Character also shows its team (for example Karasuno), school year and position (WS, MB, S or Li).

If a parameter shows a dash instead of a number, that Character cannot be played into the Area for that parameter. A Character with a 0 can still be played there.

Event cards

Events are one-time plays. Each Event shows when it can be played: [Draw], [Serve], [Block], [Receive], [Set] or [Attack]. You can only play it during that phase of your own turn. To use it, put it face up in your Event Area and do what it says. Unless a card says otherwise, you can play any number of Events in the same phase.

The play area

  • Court: five Areas where Characters are played: Serve Area, Block Area, Receive Area, Set Area and Attack Area.
  • Guts: when you play a Character into an Area that already has one, the new card goes on top. The cards underneath are called Guts. Many skills ask you to pay Guts, which means sending that many cards from under the Character into your Drop Area. There is no limit to how many Guts an Area can hold.
  • Event Area: where your played Event cards stay.
  • Score Area: your two Score cards, face down. Think of them as your lives.
  • Drop Area: your discard pile.
  • Hand: there is no hand size limit.

Setting up

  1. Decide at random who gets to choose the first serve.
  2. Both players shuffle and draw 6 cards.
  3. Starting with the server, each player may take one redraw: put back any number of cards from your hand, shuffle, and draw back up to 6.
  4. Each player puts the top 2 cards of their deck face down in their Score Area.
  5. The server starts the first set with the Serve Phase.

How a set is played

The serve

The server plays a Character from their hand into the Serve Area. They may then use skills and play [Serve] Events. The Serve Point of that Character, plus any bonuses, becomes the server's Offense Point. That is how strong the ball is. Then the turn passes.

Your turn: block or receive

At the start of each turn after the serve, you choose one of two ways to deal with the ball.

Block. Play up to 3 Characters into your Block Area. One of them is the Center Blocker and the others are Side Blockers. Add up their Block Point, plus any bonuses, to get your Defense Point. If your Defense Point is at least as high as your opponent's Offense Point, the block works, and the ball goes straight back with an Offense Point of 0 unless a skill such as Stuffed changes it. Side Blockers go to the Drop Area at the end of the Block Phase.

Receive. This is the full play, and it has four steps:

  1. Draw Phase: draw 1 card. You may play [Draw] Events.
  2. Receive Phase: play a Character into your Receive Area. Its Receive Point, plus bonuses, is your Defense Point. It must be at least as high as your opponent's Offense Point.
  3. Set Phase: play a Character into your Set Area.
  4. Attack Phase: play a Character into your Attack Area. Your new Offense Point is the Set Point of your setter plus the Attack Point of your attacker, plus bonuses.

Blocking is quick and costs fewer cards, but you do not draw. Receiving lets you draw and hit back hard, but you need three Characters.

A tie is enough to defend. If your opponent's Offense Point is 0 and your Defense Point is 0, your block or receive still works.

Losing the set

If you cannot play a Character when you need to, or your Defense Point is lower than the Offense Point, you declare Lost. You may also declare Lost by choice. Your opponent takes the set, and then there is a short break called the Interval:

  1. Both players draw until they have 6 cards in hand. If you already have more than 6, you keep them.
  2. The player who declared Lost takes 1 card from their Score Area into their hand.
  3. Effects that were still running end.
  4. The player who won the set serves the next one.

Winning the match

You start with 2 Score cards and lose one each time you lose a set. If you declare Lost when you have no Score cards left, you lose the match. In other words, the first player to lose three sets loses.

Running out of cards in your deck does not make you lose. You just keep playing without drawing.

Skills

Most skills tell you when they work with icons at the start of the text.

  • [Enter] with an Area, such as [Enter][Receive Area]: the skill happens when this Character is played into that Area.
  • "You may ... to use this skill": a cost. If you cannot do it, you cannot use the skill. If you do it, you must carry out the rest.
  • [Turn 1]: during this turn, your cards with the same name cannot use their skills again.
  • "Up to 2 cards" means 0, 1 or 2. "2 cards" means exactly 2.
  • "Original" means the number printed on the card, before any bonuses.

Do what a skill says in the order it is written. If one effect allows something and another forbids it, the one that forbids it wins. If both players have skills that happen at the same moment, the player whose turn it is goes first.

Keywords

  • Stuffed (N): after a successful block, your Offense Point becomes N at the end of the turn instead of 0.
  • Deflection (N): your opponent's Offense Point drops by N. The Block Phase ends right away and you continue with your Draw Phase, so you get to receive a weaker ball.
  • Feint (N): at the end of the turn your Offense Point becomes N, and your opponent cannot play a Character into their Block Area next turn.
  • Block Out (N): during your opponent's next turn, if a Character with an original Block Point of N or less enters their Block Area, they lose the set.
  • A Pass (N): during this turn, a Character that enters your Set Area gets +N Set Point.
  • Back Attack (N): the card enters your Attack Area and its Attack Point becomes N.

Some newer keywords do not have official English names yet. On our translated cards we call them Dump and Synchro Attack. We will update them when Takara Tomy announces the official names.
