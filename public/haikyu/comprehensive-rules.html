Haikyu!! VOBACA!! BREAK Comprehensive Rules

This is an English translation of the Haikyu!! VOBACA!! BREAK Comprehensive Rules, version 1.06 (last updated October 22, 2026), translated by Hobby Collect. The Japanese text published by Takara Tomy is the master version. If this translation and the Japanese text differ, the Japanese text applies. Cross references between rules have been left out to keep the page readable.

0. Introduction

This document is the basis for playing Haikyu!! VOBACA!! BREAK competitively.

  • The rules are divided into small numbered items.
  • One action may be explained by rules under more than one number.
  • Examples are not rules. They are marked "Example".
  • Card effects, team names and other names used in these rules do not define or guarantee the contents of future products.
  • This document is added to and changed as needed.

0-1. What is Haikyu!! VOBACA!! BREAK?

0-1-1. Setting. This is a two-player card game based on the world of the comic "Haikyu!!" and the TV anime, films and novels made from it. Players use those characters to experience a game of volleyball.

0-1-2. Number of players. The game is played one against one by two players. These rules do not cover games with any other number of players.

0-1-3. Winning, losing and the end of the game. The game ends when one player loses. The player who has not lost wins.

0-1-3-1. A player who meets the losing condition loses the game immediately.

0-1-3-1-1. The losing condition is "declaring Lost while you have 0 Score cards".

0-1-3-2. Any player may concede at any time during the game. A player who concedes loses the game immediately.

0-1-3-2-1. A loss by conceding cannot be replaced by any replacement effect.

0-1-3-3. An effect may make a player win or lose. In that case the player wins or loses, and the game ends, at the moment the instruction "wins" or "loses" is carried out.

0-2. Basic principles (rule zero)

0-2-1. These basic principles take priority over every other rule.

0-2-2. Skills take priority. If a skill printed on a card contradicts the Comprehensive Rules, other than these basic principles, the skill takes priority.

0-2-2-1. As exceptions, these rules take priority over skills: conceding cannot be replaced (0-1-3-2-1), the prohibitions on entering (1-4-5-1, 1-4-5-4-1), and outside all zones (3-1-6-1).

0-2-3. Order of carrying out a skill. Unless stated otherwise, the instructions of a skill are carried out in the order they are written on the card.

0-2-4. Order of play. Unless an instruction such as "move to the stated phase" says otherwise, turns and phases proceed in the order given by the rules.

0-2-5. Carrying out.

0-2-5-1. Carrying out means performing an action such as processing a rule or resolving an effect.

0-2-5-2. An action that cannot be carried out is ignored.

0-2-5-2-1. If an action is replaced by another action, the original action is not carried out.

0-2-5-2-1-1. Because the original action is not carried out due to the replacement, it is not treated as "impossible" when a rule or effect asks whether it could be carried out.

0-2-5-3. If something already in a state is told to "become that state", the action is not carried out and the state does not change. Conditions such as "put into that state" or "when it becomes that state" are not met either.

0-2-5-4. If an action is required 0 times, a negative number of times, or any number that does not work as a count, it is not performed. A negative number of times does not mean doing the opposite.

0-2-5-5. If a player is required to do something impossible, it is not done. If an effect requires a certain amount of an action and only part of it is impossible, the player does as much as possible.

Example: When resolving "add 2 cards from your Drop Area to your hand" with only 1 card in the Drop Area, you add 1 card to your hand.

Example: When resolving "add 1 Karasuno card and 1 Nekoma card to your hand" with only 1 valid card, you add 1 card to your hand.

However, "as much as possible" does not mean you may ignore what is specified. If an effect places a requirement on its targets and the targets cannot be chosen properly, that part is treated as impossible.

Example: When resolving "add 2 cards with different card names to your hand" with only 1 valid card, you cannot add a card to your hand.

0-2-6. Prohibition takes priority. If one effect tells you to do something and another effect forbids it at the same time, the effect that forbids it always takes priority.

0-2-6-1. In card text, "only X is possible" is treated as a prohibition meaning "anything other than X is not possible".

0-2-7. Order of acting. If more than one player is required to act at the same time, the turn player acts first.

0-2-8. Simultaneous events.

  • If several rule processes occur at the same time, they are performed at the same time, and changes in the situation or information in between are ignored.
  • If you are told to perform several actions at the same time that are not defined by the rules, they are performed one at a time and are not really simultaneous. The situation and information change as you go until all of them are done.

0-2-9. Numbers used in the game. When card information other than parameters, Offense Point and Defense Point is modified, only whole numbers of 0 or more are used unless stated otherwise or defined by the rules. A value that would be negative is treated as 0. Parameters, Offense Point and Defense Point that become negative are treated as negative numbers.

0-2-10. Numbers you can choose. When you choose a number, you must choose a whole number of 0 or more unless stated otherwise. You cannot choose a negative number.

0-2-10-1. When an effect sets an upper limit such as "up to", you may choose 0 unless a lower limit is stated.

0-2-11. How long modifying and fixing effects last. A skill effect that adds to or subtracts from a number, or that changes a number with wording such as "becomes N", stops applying when any of the following is true.

  • If it is an immediate effect, it applies until the Cleanup Step.
  • If it is a continuous effect, it applies from resolution until the stated duration ends. If no duration is stated, it applies until the Cleanup Step.
  • If it is a permanent skill, it applies whenever its condition is met.
  • If the card with the changed number is a character on the court, it applies until the card is no longer treated as a character.
  • If the target is an Offense Point or Defense Point, it applies until that Offense Point or Defense Point ceases to exist.

0-2-12. Modifying effects and fixing effects.

  • A modifying effect adds to or subtracts from a number. Modifying effects add up each time they are applied.
  • A fixing effect changes a number to a stated value. Fixing effects from anything other than permanent skills are applied in the order they resolved, and a later one changes and overwrites an earlier one.

0-2-12-1. Priority between them.

  • Continuous and continuous: if several continuous effects modify, change or fix a number, all of them apply in the order they resolved.
  • Continuous and permanent skill: if both a continuous effect and a permanent skill fix a number, the permanent skill's effect applies and the others are ignored.
  • Permanent skill and permanent skill: if several permanent skills fix a number at the same time, the one applied first applies and the others are ignored.

0-2-13. Reference language. If translation causes a difference in meaning between language versions of the rules or card text, the master version they were translated from is the reference. The master version of the Comprehensive Rules is written in Japanese.

1. Game terms

1-1. Basic terms

1-1-1. The words used in these rules are defined as follows.

1-1-2. Game. One match.

1-1-3. Player. One of the two people who take part in the game and manage its information.

1-1-3-1. The player whose turn is in progress is the turn player. The other player is the non-turn player.

1-1-3-2. In rules and card text, "you" means the player proceeding through the situation or resolving the skill, and "your opponent" means the other player.

1-1-4. Owner. The person who owned the card when the deck was built.

1-1-5. Master. The player currently using a card or skill.

1-1-5-1. The master of a card in a zone is the player that zone belongs to.

1-1-5-2. The master of a skill is the player who declared its use, or the master of the card that has the skill.

1-1-6. Card. A Haikyu!! VOBACA!! BREAK product, or a card given out for promotion, officially approved by the publisher.

1-1-7. Deck. "The stack of 40 cards prepared to play the game" and "the cards in the Deck Area".

1-1-8. Target. A card, player, zone or action specified by a rule or skill.

1-1-9. Declaration. A player stating their intention. A declaration is not part of carrying out.

1-1-10. Resolution. Carrying out the instructions of an effect generated by a skill.

1-1-11. Processing. Carrying out instructions given by the rules.

1-1-12. Action. Something a player does to move the game forward.

1-1-13. Information. Includes the details of a card, the zone it is in, the continuous effects applied to it, whether it is public or hidden, and the number of cards in a zone.

1-1-14. Trigger condition. The event written on a passive skill that causes its effect to occur, such as "when a player takes a certain action", "at the start or end of a certain phase or step", or "when a certain event occurs through rule processing".

1-1-15. Waiting state. Mainly, an unresolved passive skill whose trigger condition has been met. It also refers to a delayed effect whose resolution is triggered by a point during the rally and whose unresolved instruction has met its trigger condition.

1-1-16. Public and hidden. Public means all players can see the information. Hidden means it is not public.

1-1-17. Ignore. To continue the game as if the event or information did not exist.

1-1-18. Brackets and symbols. Text in round brackets on a card is a supplementary note that explains a skill in more detail. It is not an instruction and is ignored when the skill is carried out. Quotation marks show a card name in card text.

1-2. Progress of the game and the board

1-2-1. Words about time and the placement of cards are defined as follows.

1-2-2. Set. A unit of winning, losing and progress.

1-2-2-1. One match consists of 3 to 5 sets.

1-2-2-2. When you declare Lost, you "drop the set". When your opponent declares Lost, you "take the set".

1-2-3. Score card. A card placed in the Score Area at the start of the game to track the progress of the game.

1-2-4. Right to serve. The right to be the first turn player in a set. You can give up the right to serve by declaring Lost without playing a character card.

1-2-5. Rally. The series of turns from one player's serve until a player declares Lost.

1-2-6. Interval. The procedure between one set and the next.

1-2-7. Turn. A procedure and unit of progress. One player's go.

1-2-8. Phase. A procedure and unit of progress smaller than a turn.

1-2-9. Step. A procedure and unit of progress smaller than a phase.

1-2-10. Check process. A procedure placed at fixed points in the game and also created when the situation changes.

1-2-11. Move. To continue the game from a stated turn or phase. Moving to a turn starts from the beginning of its first phase. Moving to a phase starts from the beginning of that phase.

1-2-12. Zone. A place referred to in the game, mainly where cards are put.

1-2-13. Hand. The cards in your hand, or the state of a card being in your hand.

1-2-14. Character. The character card on top in each Area of the court.

1-2-14-1. A character is named after its Area: Serve character (Serve Area), Block character (Block Area), Receive character (Receive Area), Set character (Set Area), Attack character (Attack Area).

1-2-14-2. The Center Blocker is the Block Area character placed in the Center Blocker position.

1-2-14-3. A Side Blocker is a Block Area character placed in a Side Blocker position.

1-2-15. Guts. The cards under a character in each Area of the court.

1-2-15-1. Any player may freely look at Guts cards.

1-2-15-2. You may freely change the order of the Guts within one of your own Areas.

1-2-15-2-1. Information printed on a Guts card is ignored, except when an effect refers to the information of Guts cards. Skills worded "if this is under a card", "when this is placed under a card" or "when a card enters on top of this card" are not ignored when they resolve and are treated as meeting their condition.

1-2-15-3. When the top card, treated as the character, moves, the cards under it do not leave the zone. Continuous effects that applied to the old character do not carry over to the former Guts card that is now on top.

1-3. Card information

1-3-1. Words and icons about the information printed on cards are defined as follows.

1-3-2. Parameters. The numbers printed on a character card that show its value in the game.

  • Serve Point is mainly used when the card is the Serve character.
  • Block Point is mainly used when the card is a Block character.
  • Receive Point is mainly used when the card is the Receive character.
  • Set Point is mainly used when the card is the Set character.
  • Attack Point is mainly used when the card is the Attack character.

1-3-2-1. If a parameter shows "－", the card does not have that parameter.

1-3-2-1-1. "－" is not 0. It cannot be compared with any number.

1-3-2-1-2. "－" is not affected by effects that add or subtract.

1-3-2-2. A card cannot enter an Area if the parameter for that Area shows "－".

1-3-3. Skill. Text in the text box that gives an instruction.

  • One skill is the wording in the text box of one card. Some skill wording is shown as icons.
  • There are four kinds of skill: permanent, passive, active and event.

1-3-3-1. Permanent skill. A skill that keeps producing an effect for as long as its condition is met.

1-3-3-2. Passive skill. A skill that enters the waiting state when its trigger condition is met and produces its effect automatically in a check process.

1-3-3-2-1. A passive skill has a trigger condition in its text. Some trigger conditions are shown as the [Enter] icon. Others are written out, such as "when your Attack character enters", "whenever your Attack character enters" or "at the end of the turn".

1-3-3-3. Active skill. A skill that produces its effect when a player declares its use during a Free Step.

1-3-3-3-1. On a character card, an active skill has a use timing icon.

1-3-3-4. Event skill. A skill that produces its effect when a player plays the Event card it is printed on during a Free Step.

1-3-3-4-1. Event skills are the skills printed on Event cards.

1-3-3-5. Supplementary text. Wording in round brackets inside a skill. It explains the skill.

1-3-3-5-1. Supplementary text restates the instruction before it in more concrete terms. When the effect is carried out, the supplementary text is ignored and only the rest of the skill is carried out.

1-3-3-6. Annotation. Wording below a line at the bottom of the text box that explains a term or keyword.

1-3-3-6-1. An annotation is not a skill.

1-3-3-7. ▶ This symbol marks the options of a choice in a skill.

1-3-4. Effect. The instruction produced by a skill. Effects are immediate, continuous or replacement effects, and have the nature of at least one of these.

1-3-4-1. Immediate effect. An effect whose instruction is carried out during resolution and then ends. "Draw 1 card" and "discard this card" are immediate effects.

1-3-4-2. Continuous effect. An effect that stays in force for a period. A stated duration applies to the instruction up to the end of the sentence that contains it.

1-3-4-3. Replacement effect. An effect that, when an event would occur, has another event carried out in its place. A skill worded "if A would happen, B happens instead" produces a replacement effect.

1-3-4-4. Delayed effect. An effect that tells you to resolve part of it later, when a trigger condition is met. Immediate, continuous and replacement effects can all have this nature.

1-3-5. Skill cost. The price that must be paid at the same time as declaring the use of an active skill or event skill.

1-3-5-1. If a skill has a skill cost, you must carry out the cost when you declare its use. If you cannot, you cannot declare the use of that skill.

1-3-5-2. A skill cost is written with square brackets and a colon: [ ]: . The wording inside the brackets is the cost.

1-3-6. Attributes. The features of a card: team, school year and position.

1-3-7. Area icons. Icons attached to a skill.

1-3-7-1. A skill with one of these icons meets its use condition or trigger condition, and produces its effect, when the card that has it is in the stated place.

  • [Serve Area]: when the card is in the Serve Area.
  • [Block Area]: when the card is in the Block Area.
  • [Receive Area]: when the card is in the Receive Area.
  • [Set Area]: when the card is in the Set Area.
  • [Attack Area]: when the card is in the Attack Area.
  • [Court]: when the card is in any zone that belongs to the court.
  • [Hand]: when the card is in your hand.

1-3-8. Use timing icons. Icons attached to an active skill.

1-3-8-1. An active skill with one of these icons can be declared only during the Free Step of the stated phase: [Serve Phase], [Block Phase], [Draw Phase], [Receive Phase], [Set Phase] or [Attack Phase].

Trigger condition icons. Icons attached to a skill that show a certain action or situation is its trigger condition. [Enter]: the trigger condition is met, and the effect is produced, when the card that has the skill enters.

1-4. Basic actions

1-4-1. Basic actions are the common actions that rules and skill effects tell you to take. They are defined as follows.

1-4-2. Draw a card. To move a card from your deck to your hand. Only the wording "draw" counts as drawing. A card added to your hand from any zone by another action is not treated as drawing.

1-4-2-1. When told to "draw 1 card", the player moves the top card of their own deck to their hand without showing it to the other player.

1-4-3. Shuffle. To put a group of cards into a random order that no player knows.

1-4-3-1. When told to shuffle a zone, all hidden cards in that zone are shuffled as one group.

1-4-3-1-1. If the group has 0 or 1 cards, the shuffle is an action that cannot be carried out.

1-4-3-2. The shuffle is done by the master of the zone the cards are in, whoever the master of the effect is.

1-4-4. Choose. When a skill tells you to choose a target, follow these rules.

1-4-4-1. When you choose. You choose the target at the point the instruction is reached while carrying out the skill. This is also true when a skill does not say "choose" but needs a particular card to be specified.

Example: If a skill says "1 of your Block characters", you choose that card when you carry out that instruction.

1-4-4-2. How many you choose.

  • If a number is stated, you must choose targets up to that number as far as possible. You cannot decline to choose when you are able to.
  • If it says "up to", you may choose any number from 0 to the stated number.
  • If a number is stated and only some can be chosen, choose as many as possible and resolve the effect on them.

1-4-4-3. Choosing cards in a hidden zone.

  • When choosing a card with stated information from a group of hidden cards, you look at the faces of those cards and choose. You must then show the chosen cards to your opponent.
  • Hidden cards are not guaranteed to have the information. A player choosing from a hidden zone may choose not to pick a card even if a card there meets the condition.

1-4-4-4. When no target can be chosen. If a number is stated but no target can be chosen, no target is chosen. Every effect that involves that target is ignored.

1-4-5. Play a character card (enter). To put a character card into the Serve Area, Block Area, Receive Area, Set Area or Attack Area as a character. Only the wording "enter" counts as entering. These are not treated as entering:

  • A character card being put into an Area of the court by another action.
  • A character leaving the zone so that the Guts card under it becomes the character.

1-4-5-1. A card cannot enter an Area if the parameter for that Area shows "－".

1-4-5-2. An effect may make a character card enter from a zone other than your hand.

1-4-5-3. Entering does not always mean the card changes zones. A Guts card may enter in the same Area it is already in.

1-4-5-4. Normal entry. During the Enter Step of the Serve, Block, Receive, Set or Attack Phase, the turn player may put a character card from their hand into the stated Area of the court.

1-4-5-4-1. Show the card from your hand and declare the entry.

  • Serve Phase: the Serve Area, 1 card.
  • Block Phase: the Block Area, 1 to 3 cards. You cannot play two or more character cards with the same card name. If you play more than one, one of them becomes the Center Blocker and the others become Side Blockers. If an effect prevents you from placing a Center Blocker, you can place at most 2 characters, and they are Side Blockers.
  • Receive Phase: the Receive Area, 1 card.
  • Set Phase: the Set Area, 1 card. It cannot have the same card name as your Receive character.
  • Attack Phase: the Attack Area, 1 card. It cannot have the same card name as your Set character.

1-4-5-4-2. If the Area already has cards, put the entering character on top, except in the Block Area. In the Block Area only the Center Blocker is put on top. Side Blockers are placed without stacking them on other character cards.

1-4-6. Play an Event card. To put an Event card on top of the Event Area in order to use the skill printed on it. Only the wording "play" counts as playing. An Event card put into the Event Area by another action is not treated as played.

1-4-6-1. An effect may let you play an Event card from a zone other than your hand.

1-4-6-2. Normal play. During the Free Step of the Serve, Block, Draw, Receive, Set or Attack Phase, the turn player may play an Event card from their hand, as follows.

  1. Put 1 Event card from your hand into the Event Area and declare that you play it. The play timing icon on the card shows the phases in which it can be played.
  2. If the Event Area already has cards, put the played card on top.
  3. Declare the use of the skill.
  4. Carry out the instructions of the effect.

1-4-7. Discard. To move the stated card from its zone to the Drop Area.

1-4-8. Pay N Guts. To move N Guts cards from the stated Area to the Drop Area.

1-4-8-1. Unless stated otherwise, you choose any cards from the Guts under the card that has the skill asking for the payment.

1-4-8-2. If the payment places a requirement on the Guts, the information on the Guts cards is checked.

1-4-9. Declare Lost. The turn player ends the current rally and drops the set.

1-4-9-1. If the turn player does not play a character card during the Enter Step, they automatically declare Lost.

1-4-9-2. During a Free Step, the turn player may choose to declare Lost if no rule processing or skill effect is being resolved and no passive skill or delayed effect is in the waiting state.

1-4-9-2-1. Even if a delayed effect that resolves at some point during the rally exists, Lost may be declared as long as that effect is not in the waiting state.

1-4-9-3. If a block or receive fails during the Judge Step, the turn player automatically declares Lost.

1-4-9-4. An effect may make a player declare Lost.

1-4-9-5. When Lost is declared, the turn ends immediately and the Lost Set Step is carried out.

2. Card details

A card shows: card type (2-2), team (2-3), school year (2-4), position (2-5), card name (2-6), Serve Point (2-7), Block Point (2-8), Receive Point (2-9), Set Point (2-10), Attack Point (2-11), play timing icon (2-12), text box (2-13), illustration (2-14), card number (2-15), rarity (2-16), illustrator name (2-17) and copyright notice (2-18). On some cards the position and style of this information differ from the sample.

2-1. Front and back

2-1-1. A card has a front and a back. The side with the card number at the bottom left is the front. The other side is the back.

2-2. Card type

2-2-1. Shows the type of the card.

2-2-2. There are two card types. A character card represents a volleyball player. An Event card represents a famous scene from Haikyu!!, or a non-player such as a head coach, coach or manager.

2-3. Team

2-3-1. An attribute of the card that shows its school or team. The teams are listed in Appendix 1. A team name may be a shortened form of the official name in the original work. One card may have more than one team.

2-3-2. Skills may refer to this.

2-4. School year

2-4-1. An attribute of the card that shows the character's year as a student. The school years are listed in Appendix 2. One card may have more than one school year.

2-4-2. Skills may refer to this.

2-4-3. Some cards do not have this attribute, for example when the character is not a student.

2-5. Position

2-5-1. An attribute of the card that shows the character's position in their team. The positions are listed in Appendix 3. One card may have more than one position.

2-5-2. Skills may refer to this.

2-5-3. A word other than a volleyball position may be printed here, for example when the character is not a volleyball player.

2-6. Card name

2-6-1. The name of the card.

2-6-1-1. Card names that share some of the same letters are still different card names unless they match completely.

Example: "Shoyo Hinata" and "Hinata & Kageyama" are different card names.

2-7 to 2-11. Serve Point, Block Point, Receive Point, Set Point, Attack Point

2-7-1, 2-8-1, 2-9-1, 2-10-1, 2-11-1. Each shows the card's skill at serving, blocking, receiving, setting or attacking, and is used when the card is used for that play.

2-7-2 to 2-11-2. Each is a kind of parameter.

2-7-3 to 2-11-3. A parameter can become a negative number when effects add to or subtract from it.

2-12. Play timing icon

2-12-1. Shows the phase in which the card can be played.

2-12-2. It is an icon printed on Event cards. It is not a skill.

2-12-3. An Event card can be declared only during the Free Step of the phase shown by its icons: [Serve], [Block], [Draw], [Receive], [Set] or [Attack].

2-13. Text box

2-13-1. Where the card's own skills are printed.

2-13-2. Some card designs show skills without a framed text box.

2-14 to 2-18. Other information

2-14-1. The illustration is an image of what the card represents.

2-15-1. The card number is the number of the card.

2-16-1, 2-16-2. The rarity of the card. It has no meaning in the game.

2-17-1, 2-17-2. The name of the illustrator. It has no meaning in the game.

2-18-1, 2-18-2. The copyright notice. It has no meaning in the game.

2-19. Updates to card information

2-19-1. Card information may be updated. Updates are announced on the official website and elsewhere.

2-19-2. If the information on a card differs from the announced update, the card is treated as having the updated information.

3. Zones

3-1. Zones and moving between them

3-1-1. Each player's zones. Each player has one of each zone, of which they are the master. Zones with the same name but different masters are different zones. There are 10 zones: Deck Area, Score Area, hand, Drop Area, Serve Area, Block Area, Receive Area, Set Area, Attack Area and Event Area.

3-1-2. Each player's court. A player's Serve Area, Block Area, Receive Area, Set Area and Attack Area together are called that player's court. Each is an Area in its own right and also part of the court.

3-1-3. Number of cards. The number of cards in each zone is public to all players and can be checked at any time.

3-1-4. Public and hidden zones. A zone where the faces of the cards are shown to all players is a public zone. Any other zone is a hidden zone.

3-1-5. Moving cards. A card moves when the zone it belongs to changes. The move counts as carried out once the card is in the new zone. Unless stated otherwise, a card that has moved is treated as a new card in the new zone, and effects that applied in the old zone no longer apply.

3-1-5-1. When a card moves between zones of the court, effects that applied in the old zone keep applying unless stated otherwise.

3-1-5-2. When several cards are put into a zone at the same time, their owner decides the order unless stated otherwise.

3-1-5-2-1. If the owner decides the order of several cards put from a public zone into a hidden zone at the same time, the other player may not know that order.

3-1-5-3. How to move cards. For "move N cards": if N is 0, nothing happens. If N is 1 or more, the player repeats "move 1 card" N times. For "move up to N cards": if N is 0, nothing happens. Otherwise the player first decides and declares the number, then carries out "move 1 card" that many times.

3-1-6. Outside all zones. Anything not in a zone is outside the game. Outside the game is not a zone.

3-1-6-1. Cards outside the game are not referred to in that game.

3-2. Deck Area

3-2-1. Where you put your deck at the start of the game.

3-2-2. A hidden zone. The cards are stacked face down. No player may look at them or their order, and the order cannot be changed.

3-3. Hand

3-3-1. Where you put the cards you draw from your deck.

3-3-2. A hidden zone, but you may freely look at your own hand and change the order of the cards.

3-3-3. You may not look at another player's hand.

3-3-4. It can hold any number of cards.

3-4. Drop Area

3-4-1. Where Side Blockers are put at the end of the Block Phase, where cards paid as Guts are put, and so on.

3-4-2. A public zone. The cards are stacked face up and any player may look at them freely. Its master may change the order of the cards. A new card is put on top of the cards already there.

3-4-3. It can hold any number of cards.

3-5. Serve Area

3-5-1. Where you put your serving character.

3-5-2. A public zone. The cards are stacked face up and any player may look at them freely. A new card is put on top of the cards already there.

3-5-3. It can hold any number of cards.

3-6. Block Area

3-6-1. Where you put your blocking characters.

3-6-1-1. The Block Area has a Center Blocker position and Side Blocker positions.

3-6-2. A public zone. The cards are placed face up and any player may look at them freely.

3-6-2-1. A new card in the Center Blocker position is put on top of the cards already there.

3-6-2-2. The Center Blocker position can hold any number of cards.

3-6-3. When cards enter this Area, up to 3 character cards can be placed.

3-6-3-1. One of them must be the Center Blocker. The player chooses which. The other cards that entered are Side Blockers.

3-7. Receive Area, 3-8. Set Area, 3-9. Attack Area

3-7-1, 3-8-1, 3-9-1. Where you put your receiving, setting and attacking character.

3-7-2, 3-8-2, 3-9-2. Each is a public zone. The cards are stacked face up and any player may look at them freely. A new card is put on top of the cards already there.

3-7-3, 3-8-3, 3-9-3. Each can hold any number of cards.

3-10. Event Area

3-10-1. Where you put your Event cards when you declare that you play them.

3-10-2. A public zone. The cards are stacked face up and any player may look at them freely. Its master may change the order of the cards. A new card is put on top of the cards already there.

3-10-3. It can hold any number of cards.

3-11. Score Area

3-11-1. Where you put your Score cards.

3-11-2. A hidden zone. Unless stated otherwise, the cards are placed face down so that their number can be seen. No player may look at them, and their order cannot be changed. A new card is put on top of the cards already there.

3-11-3. A Score card placed face up by a skill, or turned face up, is treated as a card in a public zone.

3-11-4. When a card moves out of this zone, you may choose any card unless stated otherwise.

4. Preparing for the game

4-1. Preparing a deck

4-1-1. Before the game each player needs a deck built from their own Haikyu!! VOBACA!! BREAK cards.

4-1-2. Deck construction. A deck must meet these conditions: exactly 40 cards, and 8 or fewer Event cards.

4-1-2-1. A permanent skill about deck construction applies as a replacement effect that changes the conditions above.

4-1-2-1-1. These are skills worded "you can only have the stated number in your deck", "you cannot have the stated card in your deck", "you can only have the stated cards in your deck" and similar.

4-1-2-1-2. A permanent skill that gives an attribute or card name, such as "this card in all zones has [attribute]", is in force from deck construction.

4-2. Before the game

4-2-1. Before the game starts, each player follows these steps.

4-2-1-1. Present the deck you will use. The deck only has to meet the deck construction rules at this point.

4-2-1-2. Deciding the right to serve. Choose one player at random. That player chooses whether to take the first right to serve. If they give it up, the other player automatically has it.

4-2-1-3. Shuffling. Each player shuffles their deck and places it face down in their Deck Area.

4-2-1-4. Starting hand. Each player draws 6 cards from their deck as their starting hand. Then, starting with the player who has the first right to serve, each player may redraw once as follows.

4-2-1-4-1. Return any number of cards of your choice from your hand to your deck and shuffle the deck. Then draw until you have 6 cards in hand.

4-2-1-5. Score cards. Each player puts the top 2 cards of their deck face down in their Score Area.

4-2-1-6. Starting the game. The game starts with the Serve Phase of the player who has the first right to serve.

4-2-2. A permanent skill about the start of the game applies as a replacement effect that changes the stated item of the steps above, such as the number of cards in hand.

5. Playing the game

5-1. Turn basics

5-1-1. The game proceeds by repeating turns, with the players taking the role of turn player one after the other.

5-1-1-1. One set consists of turns repeated until a player declares Lost.

5-1-2. A turn consists of several phases. Steps are a smaller unit than phases.

5-1-3. The turn player carries out the phases and steps according to the rules.

5-1-3-1. If a phase or step ceases to exist or is skipped, the events in it that would be trigger conditions do not occur, the players can take no action in it, and the game goes on to the next phase or step.

5-1-4. When the turn player declares Lost and neither player meets the losing condition, the game goes to the Interval.

5-2. The three kinds of turn

5-2-1. A turn has one of three structures.

5-2-2. The first turn of each set is taken by the player with the right to serve and has two phases: Serve Phase, End Phase.

5-2-2-1. The Serve Phase happens only in the first turn of each set.

5-2-3. Every later turn in the set has one of these two structures.

  • If the turn player chooses to block in the Start Phase: Start Phase, Block Phase, End Phase.
  • If the turn player chooses to receive in the Start Phase: Start Phase, Draw Phase, Receive Phase, Set Phase, Attack Phase, End Phase.

5-3. Interval

5-3-1. The period in which the processing after Lost is carried out.

5-3-2. Interval procedure.

  1. The Interval starts. Effects that last "during the Interval" come into force.
  2. Each player draws until they have 6 cards in hand.
  3. The player who declared Lost adds 1 card from their Score Area to their hand.
  4. The continuous effects of the player who declared Lost end.
  5. The continuous effects of the other player end.
  6. The Interval ends. Effects that last "during the Interval" stop. The player who did not declare Lost gets the right to serve for the next set and becomes the turn player, and the game goes to the Serve Phase of the next set.

5-4. Check process

5-4-1. A check process is the period for resolving passive skills and delayed effects.

5-4-2. Check process procedure.

  1. Finish rule processing. If a rule process is under way or waiting, finish it. If that causes new rule processing, repeat this step until none is left. Then go to step 2.
  2. Turn player. If the turn player is the master of any passive skill or delayed effect in the waiting state, the turn player resolves one of their choice and returns to step 1. If there are none, go to step 3.
  3. Non-turn player. If the non-turn player is the master of any passive skill or delayed effect in the waiting state, the non-turn player resolves one of their choice and returns to step 1. If there are none, go to step 4.
  4. End the check process. Continue the game from the phase or step you were in.

5-5. Serve Phase

5-5-1. The phase in which you serve and start the rally.

5-5-2. Serve Phase procedure.

  1. Start of the phase. Effects that last "during the Serve Phase", "during your turn" or "during this set" come into force. Skills triggered "at the start of the Serve Phase", "at the start of the turn" or "at the start of the set" enter the waiting state. Carry out a check process.
  2. Enter Step (5-13). If the turn player cannot play a character card in this step, they declare Lost and go to the Lost Set Step. Carry out a check process.
  3. Free Step (5-14). If the turn player declares Lost, go to the Lost Set Step. Carry out a check process.
  4. Offense Point Calculation Step (5-17).
  5. End of the phase. Skills triggered "at the end of the Serve Phase" enter the waiting state. Carry out a check process. Effects that last "during the Serve Phase" or "during this phase" stop. Go to the End Phase.

5-6. Start Phase

5-6-1. The phase in which the turn player carries out the processing for the start of their turn.

5-6-2. Start Phase procedure.

  1. Start of the phase. Effects that last "during the Start Phase" or "during your turn" come into force. Skills triggered "at the start of the Start Phase" or "at the start of the turn" enter the waiting state. Carry out a check process.
  2. Choose block or receive. The turn player chooses and declares whether they will carry out the Block Phase or the Receive Phase this turn.
  3. End of the phase. Skills triggered "at the end of the Start Phase" enter the waiting state. Carry out a check process. Effects that last "during the Start Phase" stop. If block was chosen, go to the Block Phase. If receive was chosen, go to the Draw Phase.

5-7. Block Phase

5-7-1. The phase in which the turn player blocks.

5-7-2. Block Phase procedure.

  1. Start of the phase. Effects that last "during the Block Phase" come into force. Skills triggered "at the start of the Block Phase" enter the waiting state. Carry out a check process.
  2. Enter Step (5-13). If the turn player does not play a character card in this step, they declare Lost and go to the Lost Set Step. Carry out a check process.
  3. Free Step (5-14). If the turn player declares Lost, go to the Lost Set Step. Carry out a check process.
  4. Defense Point Calculation Step (5-18).
  5. Judge Step. If the block fails in this step, the turn player declares Lost and goes to the Lost Set Step.
  6. Offense Point Calculation Step (5-17).
  7. End of the phase. Move all Side Blockers to the Drop Area. Skills triggered "at the end of the Block Phase" enter the waiting state. Carry out a check process. Effects that last "during the Block Phase" or "during this phase" stop. Go to the End Phase.

5-8. Draw Phase

5-8-1. The phase in which you add a card from your deck to your hand.

5-8-2. Draw Phase procedure.

  1. Start of the phase. Effects that last "during the Draw Phase" come into force. Skills triggered "at the start of the Draw Phase" enter the waiting state. Carry out a check process.
  2. Draw Step (5-16). Carry out a check process.
  3. Free Step (5-14). If the turn player declares Lost, go to the Lost Set Step. Carry out a check process.
  4. End of the phase. Skills triggered "at the end of the Draw Phase" enter the waiting state. Carry out a check process. Effects that last "during the Draw Phase" stop. Go to the Receive Phase.

5-9. Receive Phase

5-9-1. The phase in which the turn player receives.

5-9-2. Receive Phase procedure.

  1. Start of the phase. Effects that last "during the Receive Phase" come into force. Skills triggered "at the start of the Receive Phase" enter the waiting state. Carry out a check process.
  2. Enter Step (5-13). If the turn player does not play a character card in this step, they declare Lost and go to the Lost Set Step. Carry out a check process.
  3. Free Step (5-14). If the turn player declares Lost, go to the Lost Set Step. Carry out a check process.
  4. Defense Point Calculation Step (5-18).
  5. Judge Step. If the receive fails in this step, the turn player declares Lost and goes to the Lost Set Step.
  6. End of the phase. Skills triggered "at the end of the Receive Phase" enter the waiting state. Carry out a check process. Effects that last "during the Receive Phase" or "during this phase" stop. Go to the Set Phase.

5-10. Set Phase

5-10-1. The phase in which the turn player sets.

5-10-2. Set Phase procedure.

  1. Start of the phase. Effects that last "during the Set Phase" come into force. Skills triggered "at the start of the Set Phase" enter the waiting state. Carry out a check process.
  2. Enter Step (5-13). If the turn player does not play a character card in this step, they declare Lost and go to the Lost Set Step. Carry out a check process.
  3. Free Step (5-14). If the turn player declares Lost, go to the Lost Set Step. Carry out a check process.
  4. End of the phase. Skills triggered "at the end of the Set Phase" enter the waiting state. Carry out a check process. Effects that last "during the Set Phase" or "during this phase" stop. Go to the Attack Phase.

5-11. Attack Phase

5-11-1. The phase in which the turn player attacks.

5-11-2. Attack Phase procedure.

  1. Start of the phase. Effects that last "during the Attack Phase" come into force. Skills triggered "at the start of the Attack Phase" enter the waiting state. Carry out a check process.
  2. Enter Step (5-13). If the turn player does not play a character card in this step, they declare Lost and go to the Lost Set Step. Carry out a check process.
  3. Free Step (5-14). If the turn player declares Lost, go to the Lost Set Step. Carry out a check process.
  4. Offense Point Calculation Step (5-17).
  5. End of the phase. Skills triggered "at the end of the Attack Phase" enter the waiting state. Carry out a check process. Effects that last "during the Attack Phase" or "during this phase" stop. Go to the End Phase.

5-12. End Phase

5-12-1. The phase in which the processing for the end of the turn is carried out.

5-12-2. End Phase procedure.

  1. Start of the phase. Effects that last "during the End Phase" come into force. A skill already in force does not come into force a second time. Skills triggered "at the start of the End Phase" or "at the end of the turn" enter the waiting state. A skill that has already entered the waiting state once this turn does not enter it again. Carry out a check process. If any skill triggered "at the end of the turn" has not yet entered the waiting state this turn, repeat this step. Otherwise go to step 2.
  2. Cleanup Step (5-19).
  3. End of the phase. Carry out a check process. End the turn. The current non-turn player becomes the turn player and the game goes to the Start Phase of the next turn.

5-13. Enter Step

5-13-1. The step in which a character card is played from your hand onto the court.

5-13-2. Enter Step procedure.

  1. Entry. The turn player plays a character card from their hand onto the court. Each phase has its own conditions for this. If the turn player does not play a character card, they declare Lost and go to the Lost Set Step. Carry out a check process.
  2. End of the step. End this step and go to the Free Step.

5-14. Free Step

5-14-1. The step in which you use the active skills of character cards and the event skills of Event cards.

5-14-2. A Free Step is carried out only if the Draw Step or Enter Step just before it reached its "end of the step". If for some reason the previous step did not, the Free Step of that phase is not carried out.

5-14-3. Free Step procedure.

  1. Check process. Carry out a check process.
  2. The turn player may do one of the following: use an active skill of a character card, then return to step 1 after it resolves. Play an Event card and use its skill, then return to step 1 after it resolves. Declare Lost and go to the Lost Set Step. Do nothing and go to step 3.
  3. End of the step. End this step.

5-15. Judge Step

5-15-1. The step in which your opponent's Offense Point is compared with your Defense Point to see whether the defense succeeds.

5-15-2. Judge Step procedure.

  1. Judging the defense. Compare your opponent's Offense Point with your Defense Point and check whether the block or receive succeeds.
  2. Carry out a check process.
  3. Offense Point and Defense Point cease to exist. If the block or receive succeeded in step 1, go to step 4. If it failed, the turn player declares Lost and goes to the Lost Set Step.
  4. End of the step. End this step.

5-15-3. When a defense fails.

  • In the Block Phase, the block fails if the Defense Point is lower than the Offense Point.
  • In the Receive Phase, the receive fails if the Defense Point is lower than the Offense Point.
  • An effect may have the defense judged by other conditions.
  • If there is more than one failure condition, the defense fails if any one of them is met.

5-16. Draw Step

5-16-1. The step in which you add a card from your deck to your hand.

5-16-2. Draw Step procedure.

  1. The turn player draws 1 card from their deck.
  2. End this step and go to the Free Step.

5-17. Offense Point Calculation Step

5-17-1. The step in which you provisionally calculate and declare your Offense Point.

5-17-2. Offense Point. A number that shows the power of the ball and the quality of the attack when you send the ball back to your opponent.

5-17-3. Offense Point of a serve. If you carried out the Serve Phase this turn, your Offense Point is the Serve Point of your Serve character, with modifications and changes applied.

5-17-4. Offense Point of a block. If you carried out the Block Phase this turn, your Offense Point is 0, with modifications and changes applied.

5-17-5. Offense Point of an attack. If you carried out the Attack Phase this turn, your Offense Point is the Set Point of your Set character plus the Attack Point of your Attack character, with modifications and changes applied.

5-17-6. The calculated Offense Point is the basis for the Offense Point until the end of the Judge Step of your opponent's next turn.

5-17-7. After the Offense Point Calculation Step ends, the Offense Point is independent of the parameters of the characters it was calculated from. If those parameters change, the calculated Offense Point does not.

5-18. Defense Point Calculation Step

5-18-1. The step in which you provisionally calculate and declare your Defense Point.

5-18-2. Defense Point. A number that shows the quality of your defense when you take the ball from your opponent.

5-18-3. Defense Point of a block. If you carried out the Block Phase this turn, your Defense Point is the Block Point of your Block character, with modifications and changes applied. If you have more than one Block character, it is the total Block Point of all your Block characters, with modifications and changes applied.

5-18-4. Defense Point of a receive. If you carried out the Receive Phase this turn, your Defense Point is the Receive Point of your Receive character, with modifications and changes applied.

5-18-5. The calculated Defense Point is the basis for the Defense Point until the end of the Judge Step of that turn.

5-18-6. After the Defense Point Calculation Step ends, the Defense Point is independent of the parameters of the characters it was calculated from. If those parameters change, the calculated Defense Point does not.

5-19. Cleanup Step

5-19-1. The step in which the end of continuous effects is checked.

5-19-2. Continuous effects end unless they state a duration.

5-19-3. Effects that modify or fix numbers such as parameters end.

5-20. Lost Set Step

5-20-1. The step that ends the set after Lost.

5-20-2. The turn player for this step is the player who was the turn player when Lost was declared.

5-20-3. Lost Set Step procedure.

  1. Any Offense Point or Defense Point that exists ceases to exist.
  2. Start of the step. Skills triggered "when you declare Lost" or "when you Lost" enter the waiting state. Carry out a check process.
  3. Effects during the set end. Continuous effects that last until some point in the set stop.
  4. Waiting states in the set cease. The waiting state of any passive skill that met its trigger condition during the set, and any delayed effect that resolves at some point in the set, ceases to exist. Its instruction is not resolved.
  5. End of the step. End this step and go to the Interval.

6. Using skills and resolving effects

The conditions and method for resolving an effect depend on the kind of skill.

6-1. Valid and invalid skills

6-1-1. Valid skill. A skill is valid when none of it has been made invalid. A skill that can be used, or that can produce its effect, is valid. A skill that can only be used under a certain condition is still valid when that condition is not met, as long as it has not been made invalid. It simply cannot be used for lack of the condition. Being valid does not guarantee that a skill can be used or that its effect will occur.

6-1-2. Invalid skill. All or part of a skill made invalid by an effect.

  • If all of a skill is invalid, its master cannot declare its use and it does not produce an effect. Any choice it would ask for is not made.
  • If part of a skill is invalid, that part does not produce an effect. Any choice that part would ask for is not made.

6-1-2-1. If an original skill printed on a card becomes invalid, it produces no effect, but it still exists.

6-1-2-2. If a skill granted by another skill becomes invalid, it ceases to exist.

6-1-3. Cards with invalid skills. A card whose skills have all been made invalid, with nothing more specified, cannot gain new skills.

6-2. Rules common to all skills

6-2-1. As a rule, the effect of one skill is not resolved in the middle of resolving the effect of another.

6-2-1-1. However, with a delayed effect, other skills may be declared or resolved between its declaration and its resolution.

6-2-1-2. If an effect tells you to resolve the effect of another skill, that effect is resolved during the resolution of the skill that told you to.

6-2-2. Skill and skill cost. If a skill or rule prevents a skill from being used, the skill cost of that skill cannot be paid.

6-2-3. Use and resolution. If a skill or rule means you "cannot declare the use of" a skill, the effect of that skill is not resolved.

6-2-4. Use conditions. Some skills work only when the card that has them is in a stated zone or under a stated condition. Such a skill can be used only while the card meets that condition. Otherwise its use cannot be declared and its effect does not occur.

6-3. Skills and instructions that have already occurred

6-3-1. An instruction from an effect that has already resolved and occurred is independent of its source. If the source skill later becomes invalid, or the source card changes zones, the resolution of that instruction is not affected.

6-3-1-1. This mainly concerns active and passive skills. An instruction from a permanent skill, which is produced all the time, stops having effect as soon as its source becomes invalid or its condition is no longer met.

6-4. Referring to a card's last information

6-4-1. Last information. The information of a card in the most recent situation that can be checked in the game.

6-4-2. When an effect refers to the information or state of a card as it is carried out, it uses that card's last information. If the card has moved to a hidden zone where it cannot be checked, or has been removed from the game, the information from when it was last in a zone where it could be checked is used.

6-5. Permanent skills

6-5-1. A permanent skill is not declared. It produces its effect at all times while its condition is met.

6-5-2. If a target stops being covered by a permanent skill because of an effect, and would be covered by the same skill again in the same turn, the skill does not apply to that target.

6-5-3. A skill that changes the card name when a character card enters is a permanent skill whose effect replaces a normal entry with a special entry.

6-5-4. A skill about deck construction, referred to when preparing a deck, is a permanent skill.

6-5-5. A skill about the start of the game, which takes effect during the steps before the game, is a permanent skill.

6-6. Resolving passive skills

6-6-1. A passive skill is not declared. From meeting its trigger condition to resolution it proceeds as follows.

  1. Waiting state. When the trigger condition is met, the skill enters the waiting state. If the trigger condition is met several times, the skill enters the waiting state that many times. This is also true when one event meets the trigger condition N times at once.
  2. Resolution in the check process. A passive skill in the waiting state is resolved automatically in a check process. If several are waiting, the order is decided by the check process rules.
  3. Declaring resolution. The player names one waiting passive skill of which they are the master and declares its resolution. One instance of its waiting state is used up. If for some reason the chosen skill cannot be resolved, that instance is cancelled.
  4. Carrying out the effect. Carry out the instructions of the effect.
  5. End of resolution. Return to the check process.

Example: A passive skill triggered "whenever your opponent draws a card" enters the waiting state 3 times when your opponent carries out "draw 3 cards".

6-6-2. Zone change skills. Passive skills triggered by a card moving between zones.

6-6-2-1. When the effect of a zone change skill needs the information of the card that has it: if the card moved from a public zone to a public zone, use its information in the zone it moved to. If it moved from a public zone to a hidden zone, or from a hidden zone to a public zone, use its information while in the public zone.

6-6-2-2. If a zone change skill was invalid in the zone the card left, it does not meet its trigger condition even though the card changed zones.

6-6-2-3. A passive skill of a character card marked [Enter] or worded "when this card enters" is treated as a zone change skill only when the card entered by moving from another zone.

6-6-3. Passive skills that are not resolved. A waiting passive skill is not resolved if, before it resolves, the card that has it has moved to a hidden zone, the skill has become invalid, or the current phase or step has been skipped.

6-7. Using active skills

6-7-1. An active skill is used as follows.

  1. Declaring the use. Name one skill and declare its use. If the skill belongs to a card in your hand, show that card. You cannot declare the use of a skill if it is certain that nothing at all would change after its effect resolves. If the skill has a skill cost, carry out the cost.
  2. Carrying out the effect. Carry out the instructions of the effect.

6-8. Resolving event skills

6-8-1. An event skill is used as follows.

  1. Declaring the play. Show one Event card in your hand that has the skill and declare that you play it. You cannot declare the play if it is certain that nothing at all would change after the skill's effect resolves.
  2. Moving the card. Move the played card to the top of the Event Area.
  3. Declaring the use. Name one skill and declare its use. If the skill has a skill cost, carry out the cost. If the skill tells you to put the Event card into a particular Area, put it there.
  4. Carrying out the effect. Carry out the instructions of the effect.

6-9. Resolving immediate effects

6-9-1. When told to carry out an immediate effect, carry out the stated action once only.

6-10. Resolving continuous effects

6-10-1. When the information of a card is needed while continuous effects exist, apply them in this order. The information printed on the card is always the base value. Next, apply all continuous effects that do not change numbers. Next, apply all continuous effects that change numbers.

6-10-2. A continuous effect that changes the information of cards in a zone applies to a card as soon as it enters that zone.

6-10-2-1. A passive skill triggered by a card with certain information entering a zone refers to the information after the continuous effects applied in that zone.

6-10-3. A continuous effect applied to a character applies until the Cleanup Step or until the card is no longer treated as a character.

6-11. Resolving replacement effects

6-11-1. When a replacement effect exists and the event it covers would occur, that event does not occur and the other event stated by the replacement effect takes its place.

6-11-1-1. The replaced event is treated as never having occurred.

6-11-1-2. If only part of an event is replaced, only that part is treated as not having occurred, not the whole event.

Example: A skill says "if a Karasuno Guts card would be discarded, add that card to your hand instead". When "discard all Guts" occurs, the replacement applies to the Karasuno Guts, so they are not discarded, but the event "discard all Guts" still occurred.

6-11-2. If several replacement effects apply to one event, the player affected decides which to apply first.

6-11-2-1. If what is affected is a card or skill, its master decides.

6-11-2-2. If what is affected is an action, the player carrying out the action, or the master of the card the action applies to, decides.

6-11-2-3. Each replacement effect can apply at most once to one event.

6-11-3. An optional replacement effect ("when you would do A, you may do B instead. If you do, C") cannot be applied if the choice cannot be carried out.

6-12. Resolving special effects

6-12-1. Effects that use another skill.

6-12-1-1. If an effect tells you to use another skill, that skill is used during the resolution of the effect.

6-12-1-1-1. After the skill used by the effect has resolved, return to resolving the original effect.

7. Standard wording

Standard wording means phrases and forms that are used often in skills.

7-1. Wording about targets

7-1-1. Omitted targets. If a skill does not state a target, an effect on a card refers to the card that is the source of the effect, and an effect on a player refers to the master of the effect.

7-1-2. Original. The information as printed on the card, with no modifications from effects.

7-1-3. Does not have [item]. Means the card does not have the stated attribute, whether or not it has other attributes.

Example: "A card that does not have Karasuno" covers a card without Karasuno as a team and a card with no team at all. It does not mean "a card with a team other than Karasuno", so a card with both Karasuno and Nekoma does not qualify.

7-1-4. Has an attribute other than [item]. Means the card has another attribute, whether or not it also has the stated one. A card with no attribute does not qualify.

Example: "A card with a team other than Karasuno" covers a card with a team such as Nekoma. A card with both Karasuno and Nekoma qualifies. A card with no team does not.

7-1-5. N cards with the same or different [information]. Take one piece of the stated information (card name, attribute, number and so on) from each of the N cards and check that they are all the same, or that none repeat. If a card has more than one such piece of information, the player may take any one of them.

Example: For "3 cards of the same team", three cards with the teams "Karasuno", "Karasuno and Aoba Johsai" and "Karasuno and Nekoma" can be treated as the same by taking Karasuno from each.

Example: For "3 cards of different teams", the same three cards can be treated as different by taking Karasuno, Aoba Johsai and Nekoma.

7-1-5-1. Unless the skill says otherwise, the master of the skill chooses which information to take.

7-1-6. Exactly the same. "Exactly the same attribute" means each card has the same attribute or the same combination of attributes, and no others.

Example: "A card with exactly the same team as a Karasuno and Nekoma card" means a card whose teams are only Karasuno and Nekoma. A card with only Karasuno, only Nekoma, or Karasuno, Nekoma and Fukurodani does not qualify.

7-1-7. All of your characters are [team]. Means every one of your characters has the stated team. If one of your characters has no team, that character is not ignored and the condition is not met.

7-2. Wording about conditions

7-2-1. If you do. The condition is that the action in the previous sentence was carried out in full.

7-2-2. If you do not. The condition is that any part of the action in the previous sentence was not carried out.

7-2-3. When you do A, you cannot do A unless you do B. You can carry out A only if B is carried out. If a rule or effect prevents B, you cannot declare or carry out A.

7-3. Wording about quantities

7-3-1. Choose N of the following.

7-3-1-1. Choose N of the listed options and resolve them.

7-3-1-2. The options follow the wording and are each marked with ▶.

7-3-1-3. If N is 2 or more, the player who used the skill resolves the chosen options in any order. The same option cannot be chosen more than once.

7-3-2. A total of N cards. Used in wording such as "up to a total of N stated cards from among all cards in zone A and zone B". You may target N or fewer cards in any combination.

7-3-3. X in parameters and skills. X is a number that has not been decided yet. If the skill does not define X, the master of the card chooses it. Every X in one skill is the same number from paying the skill cost to the end of resolution.

7-4. Wording about time

7-4-1. Next turn and previous turn. With nothing else specified, "next turn" means the turn right after this one in the rally, after the turn player changes. "Previous turn" means the turn right before this one in the rally. If there is an Interval between this turn and the turn referred to, that turn is not referred to and that part of the effect is ignored.

7-5. Wording about revealing

7-5-1. Reveal. To let all players see the information of a hidden card. A hidden card revealed by a skill becomes hidden again at the end of the resolution of that effect. If the effect includes an instruction that hides the card part way through, such as shuffling the deck after revealing cards from it, the card becomes hidden at that point.

7-5-2. Look at a hidden zone. An effect may let you look at cards in a hidden zone.

  • Unless stated otherwise, only the master of the effect may see those cards.
  • While you are looking at cards in a zone, they have not left that zone.
  • Unless stated otherwise, you keep looking at them for the rest of the resolution of the effect.
  • If the effect tells you to hide them part way through, hide them at that point.
  • If the skill gives no instruction to move the cards you looked at, randomize the cards in that zone, for example by shuffling, when you hide them.

7-6. Wording about special protection

7-6-1. Not affected by skills. The target cannot be chosen by an effect, and when the effect is carried out the game proceeds as if the target were not there. This does not matter when the card is not the direct target of the skill.

Example: If "you cannot play an S into your Set Area" is in force on a player, it is the player who is affected, so that player cannot play an S that is "not affected by skills" into the Set Area either.

7-7. Wording particular to the Japanese version

7-7-1. The following terms and conventions are used on Japanese cards and in the Japanese rules.

7-7-2. Special use of "enter". "Play a character" is sometimes used to mean making a character card into a character on the court by entering. A card that is not yet a character on the court may be called a "character", but it is handled as a character card.

Example: "During the next turn, your opponent cannot play an MB Attack character" means they cannot play a character card that would become the character in the Attack Area.

7-7-3. "You may [action] to use this skill" and "If [situation], you may use this skill". When the stated action is carried out or the stated situation is true, you may choose to carry out the instructions that follow.

  • The choice applies to everything before this wording as a whole. If you choose to carry it out, you carry out all of it and then all of the instructions after it. You cannot carry out only part of what comes before, and you cannot carry out only part of what comes after or ignore part of the effect.
  • This wording is part of the effect and shows that the effect branches. It is different from a skill cost, which is a condition for declaring the use of the skill.
  • Unless the skill says who takes the action or uses it, that player is the master of the skill.

7-7-4. Omitted subject. If a skill does not say which player takes an action, it is the player who took the action in the previous wording. Where that cannot be determined, such as at the start of a skill, it is the master of the skill.

7-7-5. Receive draw. The turn player drawing 1 card from their deck in the Draw Step, by rule processing, is sometimes called a receive draw.

7-7-6. Vobarity. Outside the game, rarity is sometimes called "Vobarity".

7-8. Wording about comparisons

7-8-1. If [card information] is the same. The compared cards have the same card information. A card that has the information and a card that does not are not compared. Two cards that both lack it are not compared either.

7-8-2. If [card information] is different. The compared cards have different card information. A card that has the information and a card that does not are not compared. Two cards that both lack it are not compared either.

7-8-3. If a card with the same [card information] as the stated card is in [zone]. A card with that card information exists in the stated zone. If the stated card has no such information, the condition is not met.

Example: "Reveal the top card of your deck. If a card of the same team as that card is in your Drop Area" means a card with the same team as the revealed card exists in your Drop Area. If the revealed card has no team, you do not compare "has a team" with "has no team". "No team" is not a team. If the revealed card has no team and a card in your Drop Area also has no team, they cannot be compared and the condition is not met, because you cannot show that a card of the same team is there.

7-8-4. If no card with the same [card information] as the stated card is in [zone]. No card with the same card information exists in the stated zone. If the stated card has no such information, the condition is met.

Example: "Reveal the top card of your deck. If no card of the same team as that card is in your Drop Area" means no card with the same team as the revealed card exists in your Drop Area. As above, "no team" is not a team and is not compared.

7-9. Wording about number of times

7-9-1. Only once. After the text up to the end of that sentence has been processed, that part cannot be processed again when the skill of the same card is processed again. This is counted for each individual card, not for each card name.

7-10. Wording about zones

7-10-1. Put on the top or bottom of the deck. Put the card in that position in the stack of cards in the Deck Area. If the deck has 0 cards, only the "top" or "bottom" part is ignored as impossible, and the card is simply returned to the deck.

7-11. Wording about moving cards

7-11-1. Swap. Card A in Area A and card B in Area B change places: card B moves to Area A and card A moves to Area B.

  • If either card is not specified, or cannot change zones, the swap is not carried out.
  • If card A was a character, card B is placed in Area A as a character.
  • If card A was a Guts card, card B is placed in Area A as a Guts card.

8. Rule processing

8-1. Basics

8-1-1. Rule processing. Processing that happens automatically when a certain event occurs in the game and is carried out without being controlled by any player.

8-1-2. Rule processing interrupts and is carried out as soon as the event occurs, even while another action or a skill's instruction is being carried out.

8-2. Checking for a loss

8-2-1. If any player meets the losing condition, every player who meets it loses the game.

8-3. A character card placed on top of a character card

8-3-1. When a new card is placed on cards already on the court, it is put on top of them.

8-3-1-1. The card that was the character becomes a Guts card.

8-3-1-2. Continuous effects given to the card that was there first do not carry over to the new card on top.

8-4. Changes of zone

8-4-1. Examples: a card changing zones because it entered, or because Guts were paid.

8-4-2. The move counts as carried out once the card is in the new zone.

8-4-3. Effects that applied to a card in its old zone cease when it moves to a zone outside the court.

8-4-4. If a card with a passive skill in the waiting state moves to a hidden zone, that waiting state ceases.

8-5. Processing caused by the progress of the game

8-5-1. Rule processing that happens automatically when a step or phase starts or ends. Examples:

  • In the Enter Step, the turn player declares Lost if they do not play a character card.
  • In the Draw Step, the turn player draws 1 card from their deck.
  • At the end of the Draw Step or Enter Step, it is decided that the game goes on to the Free Step.
  • In the Judge Step, the turn player checks whether the defense succeeds. If it fails, the turn player declares Lost.
  • In the Offense Point Calculation Step, the turn player calculates and declares their Offense Point.
  • In the Defense Point Calculation Step, the turn player calculates and declares their Defense Point.
  • In the Cleanup Step, continuous effects end and effects that modify or change information cease.

8-6. Skipping part of the game

8-6-1. Examples: moving to the Lost Set Step because Lost was declared, and the normal progress of the game being skipped by a skill worded "skip the turn, phase or step" or "it becomes a certain turn, phase or step".

8-6-2. Actions that belong to a skipped turn, phase or step cannot be declared and are not carried out.

8-6-3. No events occur in a skipped turn, phase or step. Skills triggered "at the start of" or "at the end of" it do not meet their trigger condition.

8-6-4. Effects already in existence. A continuous effect that would end during the skipped turn, phase or step ceases as soon as the instruction to skip is carried out. So does a delayed effect that would resolve at some point within it.

8-6-5. A skill being resolved. If a skill has further instructions after the instruction to skip, they are resolved in the turn, phase or step the game moved to.

8-6-6. Side Blockers. If the Block Phase is skipped or similar while Side Blockers are in the Block Area, move all of them to the Drop Area.

8-6-7. After the processing ends, the game continues from the turn, phase or step stated by the rule or skill.

9. Keywords

9-1. What a keyword is

9-1-1. A keyword is a skill that sums up a long instruction or a combined effect in a short form.

9-1-2. The items defined as keywords in this chapter are the keywords.

9-2. Stuffed N

9-2-1. A delayed effect that, after a successful block, makes it harder for your opponent to receive on their next turn.

9-2-2. It is shown as the [Stuffed (N)] icon.

9-2-3. Its trigger condition is the end of a turn in which the block succeeded.

9-2-4. When it resolves: at the end of this turn, the Offense Point of your block becomes N.

9-3. Deflection N

9-3-1. An immediate effect that weakens your opponent's attack with a block and makes your receive more likely to succeed.

9-3-2. It is shown as the [Deflection (N)] icon.

9-3-3. When it resolves: your opponent's attack gets －N Offense Point. The current Block Phase is skipped immediately and the game moves to your Draw Phase.

9-4. Feint N

9-4-1. A delayed effect that prevents your opponent from blocking on their next turn.

9-4-2. It is shown as the [Feint (N)] icon.

9-4-3. Its trigger condition is the end of a turn in which you attacked.

9-4-4. When it resolves: at the end of the turn, your Offense Point becomes N. During your opponent's next turn, they cannot play a Block character.

9-5. Block Out N

9-5-1. A delayed effect that punishes your opponent's block on their next turn.

9-5-2. It is shown as the [Block Out (N)] icon.

9-5-3. Its trigger condition is: during your opponent's next turn after a turn in which you attacked, when an opponent's Block character with an original Block Point of N or less enters.

9-5-4. When it resolves: your opponent declares Lost.

9-6. Turn 1

9-6-1. A continuous effect that limits how many times the skills of certain cards can be used in the same turn. Those cards are the cards with the same card name as the card this skill is printed on, whose master is the player who used this skill.

9-6-2. It is shown as the [Turn 1] icon.

9-6-3. When the use of a skill that contains it (skill A) is declared: if any part of skill A's effect resolved, this skill always resolves too. It resolves last when skill A is resolved.

9-6-4. When it resolves: during this turn, the skills of your cards with the same card name as the card this skill is printed on are invalid.

9-7. A Pass N

9-7-1. An immediate effect that raises the Set Point of your Set character when it enters.

9-7-2. It is shown as the [A Pass (N)] icon.

9-7-3. Its trigger condition is: during that turn, when your Set character enters.

9-7-4. When it resolves: the Set character that entered gets +N Set Point.

9-8. Dump N

9-8-1. An immediate and continuous effect that returns the ball to your opponent with the set and gets past their block.

9-8-2. It is shown as the [Dump (N)] icon.

9-8-3. When it resolves: calculate the Offense Point of your attack, and that Offense Point becomes N. The current Set Phase is skipped immediately and the game moves to your End Phase. During your opponent's next turn, they cannot play a Block character.

9-9. Back Attack N

9-9-1. An immediate effect that makes the card enter the Attack Area.

9-9-2. It is shown as the [Back Attack (N)] icon.

9-9-3. When it resolves: that card enters your Attack Area. Its Attack Point becomes N.

9-10. Synchronized Attack <team> N

9-10-1. An immediate effect that raises the Attack Point of your Attack character.

9-10-2. It is shown as the [Synchronized Attack] icon.

9-10-3. To resolve this skill, all characters on your court must have the stated team.

9-10-4. When it resolves: you may pay up to N Guts of the stated team from your court whose card names are different from the card that has this skill. If you paid Guts, 1 of your Attack characters gets +X Attack Point. X is the number of different card names among the cards you paid.

10. Other rules

10-1. Infinite loops

10-1-1. Infinite loop. A situation in which an action can be, or must be, carried out forever. The series of actions from the start of the loop until it returns to the start is called the loop action.

10-1-1-1. If neither player has a way to stop the loop, the player who is the master of the skill that started it loses.

10-1-1-2. If only one player has an option that stops the loop, that player declares how many times the loop action will be repeated, carries it out that many times, and ends it by taking one of the options that stops it. After that, while the situation is exactly the same as when the loop started (the cards in every zone are the same), that loop action cannot be chosen again during that turn, unless a passive skill forces it.

10-1-1-3. If both players have an option that stops the loop, the turn player declares how many times the loop action will be repeated, then the non-turn player does the same. The loop action is carried out the smaller number of times and ends with that player taking one of the options that stops it. After that, while the situation is exactly the same as when the loop started, the player who declared the larger number cannot choose that loop action again during that turn, unless a passive skill forces it.

10-2. Source

10-2-1. An effect may refer to the source of an instruction.

10-2-2. The source of a skill is the card that has the skill.

10-3. Which information applies to cards with the same number

10-3-1. Reference rarity. If the wording differs between cards with the same card number and different rarities, the wording of these rarities is the reference: N, R, S, Summit, P.

10-3-2. Reference card information. If the official operator announces a correction to a misprint and the actual card differs from the updated card, the Q&A and the corrected card information on the official website for that language version are the reference.
